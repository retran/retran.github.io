## Why your position is stronger than it feels

In the Netherlands, an employer can't fire you from a permanent contract overnight: it needs your consent, a permit from the UWV (the employee insurance agency) or a court ruling. So almost every layoff ends in negotiations over a settlement agreement (VSO), and your position in them is stronger than it feels on day one.

Companies know this and push with speed and fear: the first meeting comes as a surprise, the offer is "valid until Friday", employees on a visa hear hints about deportation. Many don't know the law is on their side, and HR at international companies often knows the local rules poorly too.

Every mistake in the company's layoff procedure is a legal risk with a price in euros. If you see those risks and can back them up, you don't have to ask for "a bit more": you come to the table with a calculation of what it costs the company not to settle. The guide explains the process, the tricks employers use and that calculation.

### 📌 What this guide is based on

The guide draws on the text of the law ([Burgerlijk Wetboek, Books 2 and 7](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0005290), the Collective Redundancy Act WMCO, the Works Councils Act WOR), material from the UWV and the IND, analysis by law firms and professional HR publications. Sources are listed at the end. It's written for an employee of a Dutch legal entity on a permanent contract; Section 11 covers the rules for the highly skilled migrant permit (*kennismigrant*).

## The one-minute version

1. Don't resign: your own resignation costs you WW (unemployment benefit), statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) and your leverage. Even if HR "advises" you to resign, wait for an offer (Sections 4 and 9).
2. Sign nothing at the meeting. Say "Thank you, I'll review this with my lawyer" and leave. Every agreement can be negotiated (Section 8).
3. Don't agree "in principle" in writing: a termination agreement doesn't have to be one document, and an "OK, agreed" in an email can become one. Write "I'm discussing this with my lawyer."
4. You can cancel even a signed agreement without giving a reason within 14 days, or within three weeks if the agreement doesn't mention this right (Section 8).
5. The company can't pick the "weak" performers for a layoff: in an economic layoff, people are selected by a formal selection rule (*afspiegeling*), and performance and loyalty count only in narrow exceptions (Section 2). A sudden PIP before a layoff is a warning sign (Section 5).
6. Laying off 20 or more people in one region within 3 months is a collective redundancy: the company must notify the UWV and the unions and wait a month. If it breaches this rule, you can annul both the dismissal and a signed VSO (Section 3).
7. Sick leave doesn't stop a layoff if the UWV received the application before you fell ill (Section 10).
8. On a highly skilled migrant permit (*kennismigrant*), you have 3 months from your contract's end date to find a job, or up to 6 months if you've held the permit for two years or more. So the end date in the agreement decides how much time you get (Section 11).
9. Your minimum is statutory severance: one third of a monthly salary per year worked. Everything above that is negotiable. Judge an offer by your length of service and the company's risks: with a few years of service, offers of about 6 months' salary are common in my experience, but I have no data behind that number (Section 15).
10. The company's mistakes cost it money: base your calculation on what not settling costs it, not on "how much do I want?" (Sections 14 and 15).
11. Deadlines for claims are strict: 2 months for most claims, 6 months under the WMCO. Miss one by a single day and you lose the right to claim (Section 14).
12. A post, a complaint or joining a protest isn't a ground for dismissal. Retaliation costs the company fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*, Section 6).
13. Get a lawyer before you sign. The company usually pays for them under the agreement.

## Section 1. Three ways to end a contract

An employer can end a permanent contract in three ways, against your will or with your consent. Which one is open depends on the legal ground (*redelijke grond*). The list of grounds is closed: "we need to optimise the team" means nothing until the company fits it under a letter of the law.

| Route | When it's used | Who decides | What the employee gets |
|---|---|---|---|
| Settlement (VSO) | Any ground, including "we just want to part ways" | You, with your signature | Whatever you negotiate; usually more than the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) |
| UWV dismissal permit (*ontslagvergunning*) | Economic reasons (a-ground), illness longer than 2 years (b-ground) | A UWV committee | Statutory severance, WW |
| Dissolution by the court (*ontbinding*) | Poor performance (d), employee misconduct (e), a broken working relationship (g), a combination of grounds (i) and others | The court (*kantonrechter*) | Statutory severance; up to +50% for combined grounds; fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*) on top if the employer is seriously culpable |

### Grounds for dismissal: a to i

The law (7:669 BW) lists the grounds by letter. Knowing them, you can tell which ground the company is building when it starts a PIP, complains about "the relationship" or rewrites your role.

- (a) Economic reasons (*bedrijfseconomische redenen*): jobs disappear through a reorganisation, losses or a business line closing. Route: the UWV.
- (b) Illness longer than 104 weeks, with no recovery expected within the next six months. Route: the UWV.
- (c) Frequent sick leave with unacceptable consequences for the work. Route: the court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*).
- (d) Poor performance (*disfunctioneren*), only after a genuine PIP (Section 5). Route: the court.
- (e) Employee misconduct (*verwijtbaar handelen*): violations you've already been warned about. Route: the court.
- (f) Refusal to work on grounds of conscience (*gewetensbezwaar*), which is rare.
- (g) A broken working relationship (*verstoorde arbeidsverhouding*), so serious and lasting that continuing is impossible. The court requires proof of this and of the employer's attempt to repair the relationship, usually through mediation.
- (h) Other circumstances that make continuing the contract unreasonable. Courts apply this ground narrowly.
- (i) A combination of incomplete grounds from c to h (*cumulatiegrond*). The court can add up to half the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) on top.

The law has no letter for "a reorganisation where we pick the strongest". To cut positions, the company needs ground a (*a-grond*) with its selection rule (*afspiegeling*); to get rid of a specific person, it needs d, e or g with evidence.

### How it works in practice

Departments can be cut legally only through the UWV on economic grounds. Companies almost always offer a VSO first, because it's faster and skips the UWV review and the selection rule. Refusing doesn't cost you your rights: the company goes to the UWV, the procedure takes a month to six weeks, you're paid throughout and you get the right to object. But if the company's procedure is clean, you can end up with only the legal minimum after refusing, so assess its weak spots first (Section 15, "The limits of this approach").

A company can't fire you quickly for "poor performance", because the court requires documented problems, warnings and a real chance to improve. So a sudden PIP right before a reorganisation means the company is building a different ground (Section 5).

### How a case runs in court

1. The company files a court application (*verzoekschrift*) asking the court to dissolve the contract, and attaches a file.
2. You file a written defence (*verweerschrift*), usually through a lawyer: you challenge the ground and can ask for fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*) if the company acted with serious culpability.
3. The court hears both sides at a hearing, usually a few weeks after filing. Judges often suggest settling, and many cases end with an agreement in the courtroom.
4. If the court dissolves the contract, it awards the statutory severance and sets an end date that takes the notice period into account: minus the procedure time, but no less than a month. If the court refuses, the contract continues.
5. The decision can be appealed within 3 months.

### Written consent to dismissal can cost you WW

Besides a VSO, the company can ask for your written consent to the dismissal (*instemming met opzegging*). The 14 days to cancel (Section 8) apply here too, but if the text doesn't state that the initiative came from the employer, your consent can cost you WW. Never write "I agree to the dismissal" in reply to HR until a lawyer has read the whole text.

The company can use summary dismissal (*ontslag op staande voet*) for serious misconduct such as theft, violence or refusal to work. Companies rarely do it, because courts review it strictly, but they often threaten it (for what to do about the threat, see Section 4).

## Section 2. A layoff through the UWV

The UWV procedure takes about a month to six weeks from filing, and you have a say in it. The UWV checks three things: whether the job really disappears, whether the company applied the selection rule (*afspiegeling*) correctly and whether the company tried to redeploy you to another position (*herplaatsing*). If even one isn't proven, the UWV refuses, and the company has to start over or negotiate.

### How the procedure runs

1. The company files an application through the UWV portal: the economic case, the list of positions to cut, the selection calculation by age group and its redeployment efforts. [The UWV requires](https://www.mkbservicedesk.nl/personeel/ontslag/ontslag-om-bedrijfseconomische-redenen) proof that the position disappears structurally, that is, for the long term and not for a couple of months. The company usually attaches financial statements, a business plan for the reorganisation and the new organisational structure.
2. The UWV sends you a letter with a copy of the application. The procedure officially starts with this letter, so watch the dates from that day on. Read the attachments: they show which pool of interchangeable positions the company chose and where you stand in the selection order.
3. You usually have about two weeks for your objection (*verweer*). If you can't make it, ask the UWV in writing to extend the deadline before it expires, and give a reason. Ideally, write it through a lawyer and in Dutch, the UWV's working language.
4. If your objection raises new points, the UWV can hold a second round, with 7 days each for the company and for you. Add only new points the second time: repetition doesn't help. With a second round, the procedure can take 8 to 9 weeks.
5. The UWV committee (*ontslagadviescommissie*), made up of employer and employee representatives, reviews the case, and the UWV grants or refuses the permit. [Rocket Lawyer estimates](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/nl/nl/documenten/ontslag-na-ontvangst-vergunning) the whole procedure at about 4 weeks; in practice it can take longer.
6. If the permit is granted, the company must give notice within 4 weeks, observing the notice period. It can deduct the procedure time from the notice period, but at least one month remains. The permit doesn't oblige the company to dismiss you, so VSO talks can continue after it. You can challenge the permit in court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*) within 2 months after the contract ends.
7. If the UWV refuses, the contract continues and your salary is paid. The company can go to court.

### What to put in your objection

Your objection should answer each of the three UWV checks with facts.

- On economics, show that the reason isn't structural: the work continues under another name, it's been given to contractors, the company is hiring for similar roles or the loss comes from intra-group prices (Section 7). If the company attached only slides, ask for the financial documents.
- On the selection order, list who else does the same work, at what level and with what salary and length of service. Explain why your position is interchangeable with theirs and point out errors in the service calculation.
- On redeployment, name dated vacancies at the company and in the group that you qualify for or would qualify for after reasonable retraining, and attach screenshots.
- Add your personal circumstances: a visa, illness, long service, age. The UWV doesn't refuse a permit over them, but they carry weight in court and affect the price of the agreement.

If the company hires someone for the same work within 26 weeks of giving you notice under a UWV permit, it must [offer the job to you first](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/ontslag/opnieuw-in-dienst); if it doesn't, you can claim reinstatement or fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*, Section 14). So watch your former employer's vacancies for six months.

### Selection rule (*afspiegelingsbeginsel*)

The company doesn't choose who goes. [Under the selection rule](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/nl/nl/personeel/ontslag-geven/rechtwijzer/stappenplan-aanvraag-uwv-ontslagvergunning), within each age group the person with the shortest service leaves first. The rule protects against favouritism, which is why companies often try to get around it.

- Temporary people go first: contractors, freelancers (*zzp*) and agency workers (*uitzendkrachten*), and also people receiving the state pension (*AOW*), on-call workers (*oproepkrachten*) and employees with no more than 26 weeks left on a temporary contract. If contractors still work in your category of positions while you're being cut, the company has got the order wrong.
- The comparison covers only interchangeable positions (*uitwisselbare functies*) in the same business unit (*bedrijfsvestiging*), that is, positions with similar tasks, knowledge and skills, level and pay. The job title isn't decisive: two jobs with different titles can be the same position.
- Cuts are spread across the age groups 15-25, 25-35, 35-45, 45-55 and 55+ in proportion to their size.
- If a position is unique and disappears entirely, the selection order doesn't apply; [a court of appeal confirmed](https://www.hrpraktijk.nl/juridisch/ontslag/afspiegelingsbeginsel-geldt-niet-bij-unieke-functies-ontslag-afdelingshoofd-was-rechtsgeldig/) this exception in 2026.
- The company can leave out of the selection order an employee with special knowledge or skills it would find too hard to work without ([article 11 of the Ontslagregeling](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0036599/)). It has to justify this to the UWV: what the special knowledge is, how often it's needed, who covers for the employee on leave and what a handover would cost. If the company protects a colleague this way, check whether the justification holds up.
- A CAO can let the company leave out of the selection order up to 10% of the affected employees who are rated above average ([article 16 of the Ontslagregeling](https://wetten.overheid.nl/BWBR0036599/)). If a CAO sets up an independent committee (*cao-commissie*), that committee can decide in place of the UWV and apply its own selection rules (7:671a BW). Read your CAO: only the CAO shows whether such a rule applies to you.
- [Service at group companies counts](https://www.uwv.nl/assets-kai/files/93cf060c-dfea-4f04-85af-d12178c85e96/uitvoeringsregels-ontslag-om-bedrijfseconomisch-e-redenen-juli-2025.pdf) if the gaps between contracts were 6 months or less and the group companies were successive employers for that work, or employees regularly move between them. HR often forgets this. Service on temporary contracts before the permanent one usually counts too.

When a whole team closes, the company can't lay off a member only for the closure if they essentially do the same work as colleagues on other teams. The company has to compare them with all interchangeable positions in the business unit, and the person with the shortest service in the age group goes, even if their team carries on. Build your objection on that comparison.

### Redeployment (*herplaatsing*)

Before dismissing you, the company must look for a suitable vacancy, including one that needs retraining within a reasonable time and one [at any company in the group](https://www.awvn.nl/reorganiseren/blog/herplaatsing-bij-ontslag-nieuwe-uitvoeringregels-uwv/). The reasonable period for redeployment equals your notice period and runs from the date of the UWV decision, so a vacancy that appears in that period must be offered too.

- Suitable work matches your education, experience and abilities, and you can be trained for it within a reasonable time, usually a few months. A position one level lower can be suitable too.
- Keep dated screenshots of the company's and the group's vacancies, apply in writing and keep the replies. Use any refusal to consider you without a reason in your objection.

Turning down a suitable vacancy weakens your position at the UWV and in negotiations. So answer an offer in writing and on the merits: why it's suitable or not, for example a third less pay, a different profession or a move to another country.

### Merging departments before a layoff

Before a layoff, a company sometimes merges departments or teams into one business unit with a new structure and new job titles. The merger changes the pool in which the selection order is calculated: a larger unit has more people to compare, and new job descriptions can make positions "unique" or "redundant".

- Merging departments is a reorganisation, so the works council (OR) has the right to advise on it before the decision. If the merger and the layoff are parts of one plan, the advice must cover the whole plan (Section 7).
- The UWV checks the selection order against the actual work, not the org chart. If you do the same work after the merger, compare yourself with everyone who does the same work in the new unit.
- If the new structure has a role similar to yours, the company must offer it to you before dismissing you. Social plans (*sociaal plan*) often set the principle that the person follows the work (*mens volgt werk*): if your position essentially remains, you move into it without competing for it.
- If departments are merged shortly before the UWV application and people are moved around at the same time, that suggests the structure was redrawn to get the layoff list the company wanted. Keep the old and new org charts with dates, and the job descriptions.

## Section 3. Collective redundancy: WMCO, unions, OR

If a company wants to dismiss 20 or more people within 3 months in one UWV work region, the Collective Redundancy Act (WMCO) applies. The regions are large, usually one or more provinces, so a large layoff easily crosses the threshold. A breach of the WMCO lets employees annul both the dismissals and the signed agreements, which makes it their strongest procedural lever.

- The threshold counts every dismissal for economic reasons: [according to the UWV](https://www.uwv.nl/werkgevers/werkgever-en-ontslag/ik-wil-ontslag-aanvragen/detail/collectief-ontslag), that covers mutual-consent agreements, UWV dismissals and court dismissals. So the company can't dodge the threshold by splitting people into "20 VSOs".
- The company must report its intention to the UWV and invite the unions to consultations, [even if it has no CAO](https://www.awvn.nl/reorganiseren/blog/wmco-van-toepassing/). A complete notification states the reasons, the number and categories of people to be cut, the selection criteria and the timeline.
- After a complete notification, the company [can neither dismiss anyone nor sign termination agreements for a month](https://khn.nl/kennis/collectief-ontslag-om-bedrijfseconomische-redenen-hoe-zit-dat), unless the unions agree to the layoff in writing.
- If there was no notification, union consultation or waiting month, an employee can within 6 months [annul both the dismissal and a signed VSO](https://www.awvn.nl/reorganiseren/hr-van-a-tot-z/wet-melding-collectief-ontslag/). Invoking the breach in writing is enough; the case goes to court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*) only if the company disputes it. If waiting would endanger the business itself, the company can ask the UWV for an exception.

### How to check whether the company complies with the WMCO

1. Count how many people in your UWV region are leaving for economic reasons within 3 months, including those offered a VSO. Rumours in chats, farewell messages and managers' announcements all help.
2. Ask HR in writing whether a notification has been filed with the UWV, when the UWV confirmed it complete and which unions were invited.
3. Ask a union: FNV, CNV or De Unie receive the notification and the invitation to consultations, even if you're not a member, and often know more than HR is willing to say.
4. Ask the OR, which receives a copy of the notification and the reorganisation documents.
5. Compare the dates. If your VSO was signed before a month had passed since the complete notification, or there was no notification at all, take it to your lawyer.

If you're asked to sign a VSO "right now" and no collective redundancy has been officially announced, ask your lawyer whether that breaches the WMCO.

### The works council (OR)

A company with 50 or more people must have an OR. The OR has the right to advise on a reorganisation before the decision is made, and [the UWV won't process an application until the company has consulted the OR](https://www.rendement.nl/or-en-arbeidsvoorwaarden/nieuws/samenwerking-or-en-vakbonden-is-cruciaal.html).

- Find out whether your legal entity has an OR and who sits on it, because in a group of companies not every entity may have one.
- Write to the OR members: they can ask management questions you won't get answers to and demand the reorganisation documents. Respect confidentiality, because OR members must not disclose some of the information.
- If the OR wasn't consulted, or was consulted when everything had already been decided, the OR can appeal the decision to the Enterprise Chamber (*Ondernemingskamer*) within a month (Section 14).
- If there's no OR, employees can [ask the court to order one set up](https://www.rendement.nl/wor/nieuws/sancties-als-de-bestuurder-geen-or-instelt.html). Electing and starting an OR takes months, but the pressure on the company starts with the first letter.

OR members, candidates and former members for 2 years have stronger dismissal protection: the company can dismiss them only with a court's consent, except when the business or the department closes, for an urgent cause for summary dismissal (*dringende reden*), or with their own consent. If you're on the OR, tell your lawyer.

### The social plan

In a collective redundancy, the company often agrees a social plan (*sociaal plan*) with the unions or the OR. It sets a floor for everyone, and you can negotiate more individually.

- A social plan usually pays above the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*), for example by a formula with a multiplier. It also pays for retraining and outplacement, helps with the job search, tops up WW for a period and sets rules for voluntary departure.
- Ask HR, the OR or the union for the text. A social plan signed with a union is often published.
- The social plan doesn't cancel your individual arguments: errors in the selection order, retaliation, illness or a visa are reasons to demand more.

A social plan sometimes includes a voluntary departure scheme (*vrijwillige vertrekregeling*). If you leave voluntarily in place of a colleague who would otherwise be cut, the UWV may judge your WW entitlement differently, so check this with your lawyer before you apply.

## Section 4. Dirty tricks employers use

The company has one legal route to a layoff and many ways around it. Most of these moves are legal and work because under pressure you react on the spot. The defence is always the same: recognise the move, pause and answer in writing.

### Before the announcement

#### A sudden PIP or bad review after years of good ones

Two or three months before the reorganisation, your manager is suddenly unhappy: a PIP with vague goals appears, and your review says "doesn't meet the expectations for the level". The company is building a "poor performance" ground (d-ground) for court or lowering the VSO price, because a person with "problems" gets offered less.

✅ Disagree calmly and in writing, ask for measurable goals and keep evidence of your work: results, feedback, thank-you messages, metrics. If you stay silent, in six months the file shows a "documented problem" and you have to disprove it. Section 5 covers your defence.

#### Notes about problems appear in your file after the fact

In the negotiations, HR cites "numerous complaints" or "conversations about performance" you've never heard of. The company is building a file for court or bargaining, and often writes the notes after the fact.

✅ Send a GDPR data request (Section 13): the answer shows when the notes appeared and who wrote them. Ask HR in writing for the complaints with dates; if HR can't name any, that suggests there weren't any.

#### Reshaping roles before the application

Your job is renamed, the role description rewritten and people moved between teams or legal entities, to make your role "unique" or build the pool the company wants and bypass the selection rule (*afspiegeling*).

✅ Record what you actually did and who you're interchangeable with: tasks, projects, areas of responsibility, shared processes. The court and the UWV look at the actual work, not the title. Service at group companies counts under the conditions in Section 2.

#### A transfer to "the team that's closing"

A couple of months before the layoff, you're moved for "an exciting new project" to a team that's then closed entirely, so you're cut "along with the team".

✅ Keep the correspondence about the transfer and its date. The selection order is still calculated across the whole business unit, and a recent transfer is an argument in your objection.

#### Merging departments right before the layoff

A few weeks before the layoff, the company merges several departments into one, rewrites the job descriptions and hands out new role titles. That way the company redraws the pool for the selection rule and decides who falls under the layoff and who ends up in a "unique" role.

✅ Keep the old and new org charts, the job descriptions and the dates. Compare your actual work with your colleagues' across the whole merged unit, and check whether you were offered a similar role in the new structure; Section 2 has the details.

#### Quiet firing

Your tasks are taken away, you're left out of meetings and projects, and your manager stops replying. The company wants you to leave on your own so it pays nothing.

✅ Ask for work in writing ("please assign me tasks for this sprint") and record the refusals, because as a good employer the company must give you work. Don't resign without a signed offer.

#### Provoking a conflict

You're accused of "disloyalty", "poor communication" or being "toxic" and pushed towards a sharp reply in a meeting or chat. Your sharp email becomes evidence for a "broken working relationship" ground (g-ground).

✅ Don't escalate in writing, stick to facts and propose mediation. Courts don't accept a conflict the employer created, but you have to prove that, so every calm email works for you.

#### Selecting the people who complained

You complained about your manager or criticised a decision, and a couple of months later you're on the list, under an "economic reason".

✅ Keep the timeline: first the complaint, then your name on the list. A court can annul the dismissal or award fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*) for prohibited retaliation (Section 6).

#### An offer to switch to freelancing or an agency contract

"We value you, let's continue with you as a freelancer (*zzp*), the rate's even higher." You'd lose your dismissal protection, statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*), pension, sick pay and, as a highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*), your visa. A freelancer can be cut off with one letter.

✅ Don't agree until an employment lawyer and an immigration lawyer have calculated what you'd lose, and then only after a VSO that pays for the permanent contract.

#### A sudden move to another city or a clearly worse role

"From Monday you'll work from the office in another city" or "Your new role is support, same salary for now." The company expects you to leave, or to refuse so it can call that "failure to perform your duties".

✅ The company can change your terms only within reasonable limits and for a serious reason (7:611, 7:613 BW). Disagree in writing, explain why the change is unreasonable, don't resign and keep doing your current job.

#### A request to "hand over knowledge" to another team

You're asked to train contractors, an offshore team or colleagues at another group company "for resilience", so the work can move before the layoff.

✅ Do it, because refusing is a violation, but record what you handed over and to whom. If the work later continues elsewhere, that's an argument against the economic case and possibly a sign of a transfer of undertaking (Section 7).

### At the meeting

#### A surprise meeting with HR and a lawyer

A 15-minute meeting appears in your calendar, often on a Friday afternoon. HR, your manager and sometimes the company's lawyer follow a script written to get your agreement while you're in shock.

✅ Listen, ask questions, ask for the offer in writing and promise nothing. You can end the meeting at any time. Useful phrases: "I'd like to have this in writing", "I'll discuss this with my lawyer" and "What's the procedure and what's the timeline?"

#### "Just sign to confirm you received this"

You're asked to sign a letter "just as confirmation". The text above your signature may say "read and agreed", which turns it into agreement with an assessment or a dismissal.

✅ Write *"voor ontvangst, niet voor akkoord"* ("received, not agreed") next to your signature, and photograph the document.

#### "Sign, or we'll fire you on the spot"

You're threatened with summary dismissal (*ontslag op staande voet*) over something minor or old: being late, an old incident, a "policy violation", to scare you into signing the VSO.

✅ Summary dismissal needs a serious reason, stated immediately, and courts review it strictly: they weigh your length of service, the consequences for you and proportionality. If one is announced, contest it in writing straight away and state that you're available for work. Never leave it unanswered: it costs you WW and the statutory severance, and you have only 2 months to ask the court to annul it.

#### "Resign, it looks better on your CV"

HR advises you "as a friend" to resign so there's "no stain" and to "keep good relations", which saves the company the payouts.

✅ Say no, because resigning costs you WW and the statutory severance. In the Netherlands a layoff doesn't hurt your CV: recruiters see them every day.

#### "This is a standard agreement, we don't change it"

You're handed a VSO on company letterhead and told "everyone gets the same one", so you won't bargain.

✅ Negotiate every VSO, because the first offer has room for bargaining built in. Even if the social plan (*sociaal plan*) fixes the amount for everyone, the end date, lawyer's fees, bonus and reference are negotiated individually.

#### "The offer only stands if you don't involve a lawyer"

"If you bring in a lawyer, the offer is withdrawn" or "a lawyer will only complicate things", so you don't learn what you're worth.

🚩 This condition strongly suggests the offer is bad, so go to a lawyer. Companies usually pay for the employee's lawyer, and the threat to withdraw is almost always a bluff.

#### Access cut off the same day

Right after the meeting, your laptop is taken and your email and Slack switched off, cutting you off from evidence and colleagues.

✅ Save your own documents (contract, payslips, reviews) and colleagues' personal contacts before any announcement (Section 16). If access is already cut, ask in writing for your documents and your data under the GDPR.

#### "Off the record, just an informal chat"

Your manager invites you for "just a chat" and discusses your "situation" without HR. The company wants words from you that it isn't accountable for, such as "he said himself that he wants to leave".

✅ Promise nothing, don't share your plans, and afterwards send an email: "I'm confirming that today we discussed..."

### During negotiations

#### A deadline of "by Friday"

"The offer is valid until the end of the week, then it gets worse" or "then we'll go to the UWV and you'll get the minimum." The company wants to avoid the UWV, the check of the selection order and the WMCO (Section 3), and to keep you from a lawyer.

✅ The deadline has no legal force. Reply in writing that you're reviewing the offer with a lawyer and will answer within a reasonable time: the UWV procedure costs the company more than a week's wait. Either way, you have 14 days to cancel after signing (Section 8).

#### Your "OK" in an email is treated as agreement

You write "looks fine overall, let's do it", and the company treats the agreement as concluded, because a termination agreement needn't be one document: an email exchange can be one.

✅ Write "I'm discussing this with my lawyer" and confirm nothing until the final text. If you've already written too much, state in writing straight away that there's no agreement until a text is signed.

#### Garden leave or suspension

"You don't need to come in while we work this out": that's garden leave (*vrijstelling van werk*) or suspension (*non-actief*). Your access is switched off and colleagues hear you're "on holiday", to isolate and pressure you.

✅ Your salary is paid in full; garden leave isn't a dismissal and doesn't oblige you to sign anything. Ask for written confirmation that it isn't linked to misconduct and that salary and bonuses continue, and agree what colleagues will be told.

#### Salary or bonus held back "until we agree"

Your salary arrives late, the bonus is "pending approval" and expenses aren't reimbursed, to pressure you through money.

✅ Demand payment in writing: for late salary the law adds up to 50% plus interest (7:625 BW). If the delay continues, a lawyer can bring summary proceedings (*kort geding*) for payment.

#### "Your holiday days get used up during garden leave"

The company writes that garden leave counts as holiday, to avoid paying for untaken days.

✅ Put the exact number of untaken days and their payout in the agreement. The company can't impose holiday without your consent.

#### Options and RSUs withheld through a "bad leaver" clause

The share plan labels you a "bad leaver", and all unvested shares lapse.

✅ Read the plan: "good leaver" and "bad leaver" usually depend on the reason for leaving. In a layoff at the company's initiative, demand "good leaver" status, accelerated vesting or cash. Compare the contract end date with the vesting dates: sometimes one more month of employment gets you another tranche.

#### Pressure on those least able to push back

People on visas, with small children, on sick leave or on maternity leave get VSOs first, because they're more afraid of risk.

✅ The selection rule doesn't change. Selecting on such grounds can be discrimination: take it to your lawyer and the Netherlands Institute for Human Rights (*College voor de Rechten van de Mens*).

#### Splitting the layoff

People are dismissed in waves of 19, through different group entities or "voluntary" agreements, to stay under 20 people in 3 months and avoid the WMCO.

✅ Agreements count too. If the waves belong to one reorganisation, ask your lawyer whether this breaches the WMCO. Another sign of a breach: the notification gives one reason and the real one differs.

### Against people on sick leave

#### Pressure through the company doctor to return early

After a short conversation, the company doctor (*bedrijfsarts*) declares you fit for work and HR immediately invites you to negotiate, to end your sick leave and put you back into the procedure.

✅ Ask for a second opinion from another company doctor or for an independent UWV expert opinion (*deskundigenoordeel*), which costs you EUR 100. Until the dispute is resolved, follow the company doctor's instructions so the company can't stop your salary.

#### "Let's discuss your future" while you're on sick leave

HR "understands your situation" and wants to "find a solution for both sides". The UWV won't permit dismissing a sick employee, so the company wants your signed VSO while you're weak, to get around that protection with your consent.

✅ Postpone the conversation until you've recovered, or have it through your lawyer. The company doctor can say whether you're ready for it.

#### Forced mediation during illness

The company insists on mediation with your manager while you're ill, to turn a medical cause into a "conflict" and your refusal into a breach of reintegration.

✅ Mediation is acceptable if the company doctor considers you ready, so ask them in writing. If you're ready, take part, because refusing without a medical reason will hurt you.

#### A threat to stop your salary

"If you don't come to the meeting, we'll stop your payments": pressure through reintegration.

✅ The company may stop your salary if, without a reason, you don't follow the company doctor's instructions or don't take part in reintegration. So follow them and keep a record, and challenge in writing any demand beyond the company doctor's recommendations.

#### Closing the entire Dutch entity

The company announces it's closing its Dutch business entirely, because then the ban on dismissing sick employees no longer applies.

✅ The UWV permit is still required, and your payments continue. Check whether the business is really closing or the work is moving to another group company, and where the assets went (Section 7).

### Against visa holders

#### "The IND will deport you immediately"

HR hints that unless you sign "today", you'll have "visa problems".

✅ Don't believe it: while the contract runs, your visa is fine, and after it ends you have 3 to 6 months to find a job (Section 11). That period starts from the contract end date, the very date you're negotiating.

#### An earlier end date in exchange for a bonus

"If you leave in two weeks, we'll add a month's salary": the company wants the layoff closed faster.

✅ Count the months of job search you'd lose, because a month still employed, with salary and visa, is often worth more than the bonus. Better: agree the right to leave early if you find a job, with the rest paid out.

#### A demand to repay relocation costs

Relocation allowance, moving costs and Dutch lessons are deducted from your payout.

✅ A repayment clause usually applies when you resign, so in a layoff at the company's initiative, demand that the company waives repayment. It can't claw back the cost of mandatory training (7:611a BW).

#### Refusing a certificate of employment

HR "doesn't issue certificates" or stalls, making it harder to change employers and get a new visa.

✅ The company must issue a certificate of employment (*getuigschrift*) on request (7:656 BW). Ask for it in writing and agree its text in the VSO.

### Restructuring as cover

#### A promise that the restructuring doesn't affect staff

The company announces a change of legal form or a new holding company and writes that staff aren't affected, to avoid questions from the OR and employees.

✅ Keep this message. It promises only that the restructuring itself leaves contracts alone, and says nothing about layoffs. If reality later departs from it, the message is evidence (Section 7).

#### The work moves to another group company, and the people are laid off

The same work, clients or IP move to another group company, and you're dismissed as "surplus".

✅ This may be a transfer of undertaking (*overgang van onderneming*): employees move with the work, and dismissal because of the transfer is prohibited. When the work moves, this is your strongest lever.

#### The operating company suddenly makes a loss

After a holding company is split off or group transfer prices change, the Dutch company reports a loss, and the layoff is built on it. The economic case is judged on the employing company, not the group, so that's where the loss appears.

✅ In your objection to the UWV, ask for the group's accounts and an explanation of the transfer prices.

### In the text of the agreement

Almost every agreement contains a full and final discharge (*finale kwijting*), a mutual waiver of all claims, and often a confidentiality clause. Signing it usually also waives claims unrelated to the layoff: harassment, underpayment, problems with your relocation. If you have such a history, price it in before you sign, because afterwards those claims are gone.

#### Full and final discharge with no exceptions

One line at the end: "The parties have no claims against each other." It also closes claims you haven't thought of yet.

✅ Exclude from the waiver your pension, shares and options, last year's bonus, unpaid expenses and everything the company owes under the agreement itself.

#### Confidentiality with a penalty (*boetebeding*)

"A penalty of EUR 10,000 for each breach of confidentiality." It scares you into silence, even about lawful things.

✅ Cap the penalty, make the clause mutual and explicitly allow yourself to talk to your partner, lawyer, tax adviser, the UWV, the IND and government bodies.

#### A one-way non-disparagement clause

You promise not to speak badly of the company and it promises nothing: you stay silent while managers say what they like about you.

✅ Make the clause mutual, and agree in writing the text of the announcement to colleagues and clients.

#### The non-compete stays in force

The VSO says nothing about the non-compete clause (*concurrentiebeding*), so it still stops you joining a competitor.

✅ Demand that the agreement cancels it explicitly. In a temporary contract it's invalid without written justification, and after a dismissal at the company's initiative, courts often don't uphold it.

#### Payment "after all obligations are fulfilled", with no date

The amount is there but no payment date, or payment is tied to "returning company property", so the money can be delayed.

✅ Set a deadline, for example 30 days after the contract ends. If the company is moving assets out, push for the shortest deadline you can get.

#### "By mutual wish", with no word about the employer's initiative

"The parties have concluded that the cooperation should end." The wording costs the company nothing and is often careless, but it can cost you your WW, because it doesn't show the employer took the initiative.

✅ State explicitly that the employer took the initiative and the employee isn't at fault (Section 8), or the UWV may refuse you WW.

If the employer acted with serious culpability (*ernstig verwijtbaar*), a court can award fair compensation on top of the statutory severance. [For a highly skilled migrant, courts also weigh](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/verblijfsstatus-als-kennismigrant-speelt-een-rol-bij-beoordeling-of-een-werkgever-ernstig-verwijtbaar-heeft-gehandeld) the risk of losing the residence permit.

### After you leave

#### Someone is hired into your job

A couple of months later, the company's website posts a vacancy with your tasks under a new title: the layoff wasn't about economics.

✅ If you were dismissed through the UWV, for 26 weeks the job must be offered to you first, even when the work goes to a contractor or another group company (Section 14). Keep screenshots: you have 2 months after the 26 weeks end to file a claim.

#### The final settlement is late

The final payslip arrives late or with amounts missing, as pressure or through carelessness.

✅ Check it for holiday allowance, untaken days, bonus, 13th month and the VSO payment. Demand what's missing in writing, citing 7:625 BW.

#### A bad reference over the phone

A new employer cools off after calling your former manager, who holds a grudge.

✅ Agree the text of the reference in the VSO and record who gives references for the company, so a reference that bypasses that clause breaches the agreement.

## Section 5. PIPs: how they work and how to fight them

A company needs an improvement plan (PIP, *verbetertraject*) to dismiss you on the "poor performance" ground (d-ground). A court will end your contract on this ground only if the company proves that you learned about the problems in time and got a real chance to improve, with support. It must also prove that the problems aren't caused by a lack of training or poor working conditions and that you can't be redeployed to another position (7:669 BW). Courts often refuse requests on this ground because the file is weak, so what's written in it decides the outcome.

### What a court expects from a real PIP

- The problems are set out in writing and specifically, with examples and dates.
- The goals are measurable: what to do, how it's measured, who assesses it and by what date.
- The duration is reasonable. The law sets no minimum, but courts usually expect several months, and a US-style 30-day PIP is almost always too short for this ground.
- The company gives support: coaching, training, a mentor, time to improve.
- Meetings follow a schedule, with written feedback after each one.
- The company acts in good faith: the goals are achievable, the outcome isn't decided in advance and the terms don't change along the way.

### 🚩 Signs of a sham PIP

- The PIP appeared right after a complaint, criticism, a request for a raise, a pregnancy, sick leave or just before a reorganisation.
- Your past reviews were good, and nobody mentioned problems before.
- The goals are vague: "improve communication", "show more ownership", "meet the expectations of your level".
- The goals are unachievable for anyone in your role, or they change along the way.
- The duration is 30 to 60 days.
- There's no support, and your manager doesn't meet you or cancels meetings.
- Your workload went up at the same time as the PIP.
- You're offered a "package" as an alternative to the PIP.
- Your rating was lowered to fit a quota (forced distribution, stack ranking). Courts are sceptical of a quota as proof of poor performance.

### "PIP or package"

Sometimes the company says: "Choose: a 30-day PIP or a package of two months' salary, decide today." That's a VSO offer dressed up as a choice: the company knows a short PIP won't hold up in court and wants you to leave on your own.

✅ Don't choose on the spot: this is a VSO negotiation (Sections 8 and 15). A sham PIP raises the company's legal risk, so the package can almost always be raised.

### How to act during a PIP

1. Don't sign agreement with the assessment. If they ask for a signature, write *"voor ontvangst, niet voor akkoord"* (Section 4).
2. Give a written reply to the allegations (*wederhoor*) within one or two weeks: what you disagree with, the facts and the context (workload, shifting priorities, missing resources). Ask for your reply to be added to your personnel file.
3. Ask in writing for each goal to be clarified: what, how it's measured, who assesses it, by what date. If the company refuses, record the refusal too.
4. Ask for a coach, training, a mentor or a lighter workload during the PIP.
5. Take part in good faith: a court may count refusing the PIP against you on the "employee misconduct" ground (e-ground).
6. Keep a log: what you did, when, who can confirm it, links to tasks, results and documents.
7. After every meeting, send a confirmation email with your version of the conversation.
8. Record every change to the goals and deadlines: goals that keep moving point to a sham PIP.
9. If the problem is your manager, ask for a different assessor, for HR to take part or for mediation.
10. Send a GDPR data request. The answer often shows when and why the PIP was decided, sometimes down to messages like "we need to manage him out".
11. See a lawyer at the start of the PIP, while your replies can still be written with a court in mind.
12. If the stress affects your health, see your GP (*huisarts*) and the company doctor (*bedrijfsarts*). Sick leave pauses a PIP, but don't use it tactically: the company doctor assesses only your health.

### What not to do

- Don't resign "so the PIP doesn't stay on your record". In the Netherlands, that costs you WW and the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*).
- Don't argue emotionally, and don't discuss the PIP in group chats.
- Don't miss meetings or deadlines.
- Don't take company data as evidence.

### What happens after a PIP

- If you pass, ask for written confirmation that the goals were met.
- If you fail, the company goes to the court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*) on the "poor performance" ground or offers a VSO. The court reads the whole file, including your replies. If the file is weak, the court refuses, the contract continues and the price of a VSO goes up.
- If the PIP covers a layoff or retaliation, the company is swapping the ground: an economic layoff must go through the UWV under the selection rule (*afspiegeling*). The swap gives you an argument in court and leverage in negotiations (Sections 6 and 14).
- For a highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*), a PIP doesn't affect the visa by itself: only the contract end date does.

## Section 6. Dismissal as retaliation

Dutch law has no ground for "dismissal for criticism". To dismiss you over a post, a complaint or joining a protest, the company needs a legal ground: an urgent cause for summary dismissal (*dringende reden*), the "employee misconduct" ground (e-ground) or the "broken working relationship" ground (g-ground). Courts review each one strictly. A court treats dismissal over lawfully exercising your rights as serious culpability by the employer and awards fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*). So employers usually disguise retaliation: a sudden PIP, a bad review, a place on the layoff list.

### What's protected

- Freedom of expression (Article 7 of the Dutch Constitution, Article 10 of the European Convention on Human Rights) applies at work too. The duty to be a good employee balances it (7:611 BW): you can criticise your employer, but you can't harm it needlessly.
- The equal treatment laws (AWGB, WGBH/CZ) prohibit disadvantaging anyone who complains about discrimination or harassment or helps a colleague do so.
- The Whistleblower Protection Act (*Wet bescherming klokkenluiders*) prohibits any disadvantageous treatment (*benadeling*) of someone who reported wrongdoing. Since 2023 the company must prove that the measures against you aren't connected to the report. The protection applies if you reported wrongdoing of public interest (*misstand*) or a breach of EU law in good faith, usually through the internal channel first.
- Article 7:670 BW prohibits dismissal for union membership or union activity.
- OR members and candidates can be dismissed only with a court's consent (Section 3).
- During a layoff you're entitled to discuss it with colleagues, organise, contact the union, the OR or the UWV and refuse to sign a VSO, and the company can't punish you for any of it.

### A social media post criticising management or the layoffs

A court judges a post on all the circumstances, not on a single phrase, and usually weighs:

- tone and content: facts and opinion, or insults, threats and falsehoods;
- reach: a closed circle of friends, or LinkedIn with thousands of followers;
- identifiability: whether the company, managers or colleagues are named;
- confidentiality: whether there's internal data, figures, plans or screenshots;
- whether you tried to resolve it internally first;
- the public interest of the topic;
- your history: your length of service, past problems, whether you were warned;
- what you did afterwards: whether you removed, clarified or apologised for the post;
- the company's social media policy, if you knew about it.

Courts often find summary dismissal over a moderate post with no prior warnings disproportionate. Insulting managers by name, threats and leaking trade secrets can justify it.

#### ✅ How to write about layoffs more safely

- Write about yourself and about facts that are already public: "I was laid off", "the company announced a layoff".
- No names, insults or guesses about specific people's motives.
- No internal data: figures, plans, correspondence, Slack screenshots, information about colleagues.
- Nothing that breaches a VSO you've already signed with a confidentiality clause.
- Wait with the post until the VSO is signed: a post in the middle of negotiations gives the company a reason to lower its offer or build a case on the "employee misconduct" or "broken working relationship" ground.
- Check the company's social media policy.
- Let the draft sit for a day.

#### If the company reacts to the post

- If they demand you take it down, remove or edit it and state in writing that you did so to de-escalate and see no breach. A court counts this in your favour.
- If they call you to a meeting, admit nothing on the spot, then send a confirmation email and call your lawyer.
- If they give you a warning, reply in writing with your position and ask for your reply to be added to the file.
- If they dismiss you on the spot, contest it in writing straight away, state that you're available for work and see a lawyer. You have 2 months to claim annulment (Section 14).

### A collective petition against management

Each signatory of a petition against management's decisions, for example against the layoffs, has the same protections as the author of a single post. Freedom of association (Article 11 of the ECHR) comes on top, and if a union runs the petition, so does the ban on dismissal for union activity (7:670 BW). Signing isn't a ground for dismissal by itself: the company still needs the "poor performance" ground (d-ground), the "employee misconduct" ground or the "broken working relationship" ground, with evidence. If the company picks signatories for the layoff list, it breaks the selection rule (*afspiegeling*), and that looks like retaliation.

A court judges a petition by the same criteria as a post. Leaking confidential figures, insulting managers by name and going to the press without trying to talk internally give the company a reason to argue a breach of the duty to be a good employee.

#### If the petition is about working conditions or the complaints procedure

If a petition touches on discrimination, intimidation, harassment or workplace safety, the signatories have stronger protection than when they criticise business decisions.

- The law treats sexual harassment, and harassment linked to sex, origin or another protected ground, as discrimination (Article 7:646 BW and the equal treatment laws). These laws protect both the person who complained and those who supported them, the signatories included.
- In discrimination cases, the court usually shifts the burden of proof to the employer if you present facts that point to discrimination.
- The Working Conditions Act (*Arbowet*) requires the employer to protect employees from inappropriate behaviour, including discrimination, intimidation and harassment, and to keep working conditions safe. The Labour Authority (*Nederlandse Arbeidsinspectie*) investigates such complaints and can demand corrections or fine the company.
- If the company systematically breaks these rules and many people are affected, the problem may count as wrongdoing of public interest. Then the Whistleblower Protection Act covers the signatories and the people who help the reporter. Check this with a lawyer or the Whistleblowers Authority (*Huis voor Klokkenluiders*) before you send it.
- Don't name victims or accused people, and don't describe cases in a way that identifies anyone. Victims have a right to confidentiality, and publicly accusing a specific person creates a defamation risk. Write about how the complaints procedure works and what the company must change.

#### ✅ How to organise a petition more safely

- Run the petition through the OR or the union, if there is one: they have a procedure and protection, and management must answer them.
- Write about decisions, not people: "we ask you to reconsider the layoff plan because...", not "management is incompetent". Don't attach internal documents, figures or correspondence.
- Send the petition internally first and allow reasonable time for an answer. Going public is justified only if the internal route failed or the issue is of public interest.
- Keep the list of signatories with the organiser or the union, not in a shared work document. The list is personal data, and the company may not collect or use it to pick whom to punish. You can complain about such use to the Dutch Data Protection Authority (*Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens*).

#### If the company responds with pressure

- If management calls signatories in one by one "for a chat", act as you would after a post, and tell the organiser and the OR.
- You don't have to comply with a demand to withdraw your signature. Reply in writing that you signed the petition lawfully and are ready to discuss its substance.
- If signatories are put on PIPs, lose their projects or end up on the layoff list, build one timeline: the date of the petition, then the measures against each signatory. A pattern across many people is easier to prove than retaliation against one.
- Act together: one lawyer, one position, one lawyer's letter on behalf of everyone. Measures against dozens of signatories multiply the company's risks from item 7 of "How to fight it".
- Report intimidation to the Labour Authority: the company must protect employees from psychosocial workload.

### What retaliation looks like in practice

| Company move | How to recognise it | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Summary dismissal over a post or a "leak" | The reason is minor or old, and there were no warnings | Contest in writing, state you're available for work, 2 months to claim |
| A sudden PIP or bad review | It appeared soon after a complaint or post, and past reviews were good | The tactics in Section 5, plus the timeline |
| A place on the layoff list | Your role is interchangeable, but you were picked | Check the selection order, object at the UWV |
| Suspension, access cut off, projects taken away | No explanation, soon after a complaint | Ask for work in writing, record everything |
| Bonus, raise or shares refused | The criteria suddenly changed, only for you | Ask for the criteria in writing |
| A move to a worse role | Its timing coincides with the complaint | Disagree in writing (7:611, 7:613 BW) |
| An internal investigation on a trumped-up pretext | The pretext is old or minor, the procedure is opaque | Demand the allegations in writing and a right to reply, see a lawyer |
| A negative reference | You hear about it from a new employer | Agree the reference text in the VSO |
| Punishing the signatories of a petition | Measures against many signatories soon after the petition | One timeline, one lawyer, a complaint to the AP about use of the signatory list |

### How to prove retaliation

- The timeline, the main evidence: the date of the post or complaint, then the date of the reaction. After a clean record, a short gap points to a link.
- Past reviews, praise and bonuses show there were no problems before.
- A comparison with colleagues shows that others weren't treated this way for the same thing.
- A GDPR data request can turn up management's correspondence about you after the post or complaint.
- Colleagues who heard what was said about your post can act as witnesses.
- Company emails that mention the post or the complaint directly.

### What courts and other bodies can do

- The court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*) can annul a summary dismissal and award salary for the period of the dispute, refuse the company's request to end the contract on the "employee misconduct" or "broken working relationship" ground and award fair compensation for serious culpability.
- The Whistleblowers Authority can investigate how the company treated a whistleblower.
- The Netherlands Institute for Human Rights (*College voor de Rechten van de Mens*) reviews, free of charge, disadvantageous treatment after a discrimination complaint. Its opinions aren't binding, but courts take them into account.
- The Labour Authority checks whether the company has a policy against intimidation.

### How to fight it

1. Don't escalate: no counter-posts, threats or emotional emails.
2. Build the timeline and collect evidence from day one, including a GDPR data request.
3. Answer every measure in writing, calmly and with facts.
4. See a lawyer straight away, or your union if you're a member.
5. Pick the channel: a confidential adviser (*vertrouwenspersoon*), an internal complaint, the Whistleblowers Authority, the Netherlands Institute for Human Rights, the court. Your lawyer helps you choose the order.
6. Watch the deadlines: you have 2 months to claim after a dismissal (Section 14).
7. Use the risk in negotiations: retaliation raises the company's risk of having to pay fair compensation and its reputational risk, and with them the price of a VSO (Section 15).

## Section 7. Group restructuring and moving assets

A group restructuring at the same time as a layoff barely simplifies the dismissal procedure, but it changes the economics around it: which figures support the layoff and who you can collect money from.

### Common restructuring patterns

Companies that run an operating business and own other group companies usually restructure along one of these patterns, or several at once.

| Pattern | What happens | Main risk for employees |
|---|---|---|
| Splitting off a holding company | The shares in subsidiaries and control of the group move to a separate company, while the operating business and the employees stay put | Valuable assets leave the company your claims are against, and its separate accounts may look weaker |
| Moving functions to other group companies, including abroad | Teams, clients, contracts or IP move to another entity, and the people are laid off | The work carries on elsewhere, so a transfer of undertaking, a breach of the redeployment duty and the 26-week rule (Section 14) all come into play |
| Changing the legal form, for example N.V. to B.V. | The legal entity stays the same, but the rules for paying out to shareholders change | Moving money up the group becomes easier, and creditor protection rests on the solvency test |

### What doesn't change

- The employer stays the same. [A change of form by itself doesn't affect](https://internetconsultatie.nl/grensoverschrijdende_omzetting/document/933) the business or the employment contracts, so your contract, your service and your rights carry over.
- So does the dismissal procedure. The UWV permit, the selection order, the search for redeployment across the whole group (including the new holding company), the WMCO and the OR's advice all still apply, and a change of form can't get around them.
- The collective redundancy threshold is counted for the same legal entity, since the employees stay in it.

### Where the risks for employees appear

- [The economic case is judged on the employing company, not the group](https://zoek.officielebekendmakingen.nl/stcrt-2020-43548.pdf), so the UWV will see the operating company's separate accounts. If the reason lies at group level, the company [has to show the whole group's accounts](https://www.accountant.nl/vaktechniek/2017/3/reorganisatieontslag-in-concernverband/).
- The restructuring may count as [a major change to the organisation](https://www.awvn.nl/medezeggenschap/awvn-werkgeverslijn/vraag-van-de-week/adviesrecht-ondernemingsraad-reorganisatie/), on which the OR has a right of advice. A company with 50 or more people and no OR is breaking the law. A newly formed OR also gets the right of advice on future layoffs.

### What leaves and what stays

When a holding company is split off, every employee claim (salary, dismissal payments, fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*), back pay after an annulled dismissal) can be brought only against the Dutch operating company, while the most valuable assets, the shares in other group companies, leave it.

| Stays in the operating company | Moves to a separate company |
|---|---|
| Employees and employment contracts | Shares in other group companies |
| Operations: development, marketing, sales | Group ownership and strategic control |
| Revenue and costs from those operations | Income from holdings in subsidiaries |
| Your claims against the employer | - |

A parent company isn't liable for a subsidiary's debts by default, and without a separate guarantee you can't collect from it.

### How the transfer method changes your protection

| Method | What's visible from outside | Your protection as a creditor |
|---|---|---|
| Legal demerger (*afsplitsing*) | The demerger proposal is filed with the KvK and published | Within a month of the announcement, [creditors can object](https://repub.eur.nl/pub/107065/Repub-107065.pdf) and demand security. After the demerger, [both companies are jointly liable](https://lawyrup.nl/burgerlijk_wetboek/burgerlijk-wetboek-inleiding/rechtspersonen/fusie-en-splitsing/algemene-bepalingen-splitsing-rechtspersonen/) for the debts left with the other, up to the value of the assets received |
| Distribution of shares to the shareholder "in kind" | Visible in the annual accounts | The board of the B.V. must check that the company can still pay its debts after the distribution. If it can't, [the directors are personally liable](https://www.accountant.nl/vaktechniek/2021/3/accountantskantoor-aansprakelijk-na-advisering-over-dividendbesluit/) for the shortfall |
| Sale of the shares to the new holding company at market price | Visible in the annual accounts | In exchange for the shares, the operating company keeps cash or a claim on the buyer, so the balance sheet doesn't empty |

### What a change of legal form changes

A B.V. has no mandatory minimum capital; a solvency test takes its place, so a B.V. can move money and assets up the group more easily than an N.V. A change of form is common corporate practice and doesn't signal bad intent by itself. But afterwards an employee's protection rests only on that test and the directors' liability.

### The 403 declaration

To exempt a subsidiary from publishing full accounts, the parent company of a large group files a declaration with the KvK that it [is jointly liable for the subsidiary's debts](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/hoe-kom-ik-van-aansprakelijkheid-uit-hoofde-van-een-403-verklaring-af/) arising from its transactions, employment contracts included.

- If the declaration exists, you can claim money from the parent company too.
- Withdrawing the declaration [doesn't remove liability](https://mtsprout.nl/leiderschap/de-403-nader-verklaard) for debts that arose before the withdrawal, including under ongoing contracts. Creditors can object to the liability ending.
- You can find the declaration and any withdrawal at the KvK.

### Does restructuring make a layoff easier?

Hardly by itself, but with other steps it can ease some stages.

- If income from subsidiaries has left the operating company and its revenue depends on group transfer prices, its separate results are easier to present as weak. If the operating business is profitable on its own, this won't help the company.
- Valuable assets end up out of employees' reach. In the worst case the group closes the Dutch company entirely or lets it become insolvent, and the holding company stays protected. When a business closes entirely, the ban on dismissing sick employees no longer applies either.

The usual reasons for such a step have nothing to do with layoffs: tax, simpler management, preparing for investment, a sale or an IPO, exemption from publishing accounts, and separating operating risk from group ownership. Growing groups with a Dutch parent company usually do this as standard housekeeping.

🚩 These signs suggest the restructuring is tied to layoffs:

- its timing coincides with layoff preparations;
- it happens without the OR's advice, or in a company with no OR;
- besides the shares, IP, contracts and revenue leave the operating company;
- transfer prices change, and the operating company suddenly makes a loss;
- the 403 declaration is withdrawn, or large payouts go to the shareholder after the split;
- functions move to group companies abroad.

✅ A neutral restructuring shows when the shares are sold to the holding company at market price, cash and revenue stay in the operating company, and the 403 declaration stays in place.

Intent shows in what happens next to the operating company's assets, prices and functions, and you can follow that through KvK filings and the annual accounts.

### Where to catch breaches

The levers run roughly from strongest to weakest:

1. If after the split the work, teams, clients or IP move to another group company and the people are laid off, this may be a transfer of undertaking (*overgang van onderneming*). By law, employees move to the new employer automatically, and dismissal because of the transfer itself is prohibited. The signs: the same work continues at another group company, and contracts or revenue move there.
2. The restructuring and the layoffs are in substance one decision, so the OR must advise on the whole plan at once, while its advice can still change something. The company breaches the OR's right of advice if the OR wasn't asked, was asked only about the formal side with the link to layoffs hidden, or was asked after everything was decided. The OR can appeal the decision to the Enterprise Chamber ([Ondernemingskamer](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/roadmap-reorganisatie-3-het-beroepsrecht-van-de-ondernemingsraad)) within a month, and the Chamber can order the consequences reversed.
3. If the operating company became loss-making because income was moved out or because of new transfer prices, challenge the economic case at the UWV as an artificially created reason. Ask for the group's accounts and an explanation of the prices. False or incomplete information in the application is a ground for fair compensation for serious culpability.
4. The company breaches the WMCO when it splits the layoff across time, group entities or "voluntary" agreements to stay under 20 people in 3 months (Sections 3 and 4). A breach gives you 6 months to annul both the dismissal and a signed VSO.
5. The company must look for redeployment positions across the whole group, including the new holding company. If you're laid off while the group has vacancies you qualify for, the UWV can refuse the permit, or you can bring a claim.
6. The company breaches the selection order when it reshapes roles and moves people between teams or entities right before the layoff, to create "unique" roles or the pool it wants (Section 4).
7. If within 26 weeks of the layoff a new hire, a contractor or an employee of another group company does the same work, you have a ground for reinstatement or fair compensation (Section 14).
8. If the operating company ends up "emptied", the creditor protections in the transfer method table and the 403 declaration subsection apply. On top of them, transactions that harm creditors can be challenged (*actio pauliana*), especially in insolvency, through the trustee.
9. The company's written promises that employees will stay in their own legal entity or that the changes won't affect their work (Section 4) are evidence in court, including of a transfer of undertaking. If reality departs from them, you can argue that the company breached its duty to be a good employer (7:611 BW) and acted with serious culpability.

Almost all of these levers get stronger when many people share one lawyer. The deadlines are short: a month for creditor objections and the OR's appeal, 2 months for most claims, 6 months under the WMCO.

### Questions to ask the company

- How are the group functions being moved: a legal demerger, a share sale or a distribution in kind?
- Was the OR asked for advice? If there's no OR, why not?
- Will the parent company guarantee the operating company's debts (a 403 declaration)?
- After the split, how are revenue and costs divided between the operating company and the group?

### How to collect evidence

- [ ] Check the KvK: the demerger proposal, 403 declarations and the operating company's annual accounts
- [ ] In a legal demerger, watch for the announcement and don't miss the one-month objection period
- [ ] Send a GDPR data request: the answer shows when you were included in the plans
- [ ] Ask the OR questions, if there is one
- [ ] Keep screenshots of group vacancies
- [ ] Keep a timeline: the dates of the restructuring, the notifications and the first conversations about layoffs
- [ ] Keep the company's written messages about the restructuring, especially any statement that employees and their work stay in the same company

## Section 8. The settlement agreement (VSO)

The first VSO offer is almost never final. Without your signature, the company needs the UWV, the WMCO waiting month (Section 3) and the risk of losing: that's your leverage. The agreement settles at once how much money you get, whether you get WW and when your contract ends, and with it your visa.

### Keeping your WW

Without "WW-proof" clauses, the UWV may refuse the benefit or start it later. The UWV doesn't penalise a VSO as such; it checks whether you became unemployed through your own fault. Clauses that lawyers usually word in Dutch in almost the same way rule that fault out:

- The initiative to end the contract came from the employer: *"Het initiatief tot beëindiging van de arbeidsovereenkomst is uitgegaan van werkgever."*
- The employee is in no way to blame, and there's no urgent cause for summary dismissal: *"Werknemer treft geen enkel verwijt; er is geen sprake van een dringende reden."*
- The contract ends because of a reorganisation or for business-economic reasons: *"De beëindiging vindt plaats in verband met een reorganisatie / bedrijfseconomische redenen."* The UWV may read words about "poor performance" as your fault.
- The end date respects the applicable notice period: *"Bij de beëindigingsdatum is de geldende opzegtermijn in acht genomen."* The UWV applies a "fictitious" notice period: [until it has passed, no WW is paid](https://www.awvn.nl/ontslag/hr-van-a-tot-z/ontslag-met-wederzijds-goedvinden/). The UWV usually counts it from the date of signing, so signing in November with an end date in the same November pushes your WW back by a month or more.
- There's no ban on dismissal, which means you aren't ill and have no other protection: *"Er is geen sprake van een opzegverbod."* If you're ill, this clause is false and the VSO is dangerous (Section 10).
- The company gives you the employer's statement for WW (*werkgeversverklaring*), which you need for the benefit application. Ask for it in advance.

### 14 days to cancel

After signing, you have [14 days to cancel the agreement without giving a reason](https://cms.law/nl/NLD/WWZ/Wet-Werk-en-Zekerheid/Vaststellingsovereenkomst). If the company didn't mention this right in the agreement, the period extends to three weeks. Cancel with a written, dated message to your employer, ideally by letter and email at the same time.

Read the agreement before you sign anyway: if you cancel, a new agreement within six months comes without a cancellation right. Companies sometimes write that the payment comes only after the 14 days have passed, and that's normal.

### What you can negotiate

If the company won't move on the amount, negotiate other items: each one is worth money or time.

- [ ] Ask for a payment above the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) as a number of monthly salaries ("6 months gross, including holiday allowance") or as a multiplier of the statutory severance. A reasonable amount depends on your length of service and the company's risks: back it up with the calculation in Section 15 (the length of the procedure, the company's risks, your visa) and check it against that section's "Market benchmarks".
- [ ] Ask for the latest possible end date, with garden leave on full pay. Months in employment give you salary, pension contributions, holiday and, for a highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*), time on your visa before the search period starts.
- [ ] Ask the company to pay your lawyer's fee, usually EUR 1,000 to 2,500 excluding VAT (*excl. btw*), directly against the lawyer's invoice. Wording: the company pays for the employee's legal assistance up to an amount of X excluding VAT.
- [ ] Ask for a pro-rata bonus for the year. If the bonus depends on targets, ask for them to count as 100% met: you can't influence them after you leave.
- [ ] Write down the exact number of untaken holiday days and their payment in money.
- [ ] Get the non-compete clause (*concurrentiebeding*) and the non-solicitation clause (*relatiebeding*) cancelled, or the VSO may stop you from joining a competitor.
- [ ] Ask the company to waive repayment of relocation, training and certification costs.
- [ ] Ask for "good leaver" status for options and RSUs, accelerated vesting or a cash compensation. Check the vesting dates against the end date.
- [ ] Agree a neutral or positive reference text, the announcement to colleagues and clients and the name of the person who gives references.
- [ ] Ask to keep your laptop and phone. Companies often let you keep the equipment: for them it's pocket change and for you it's convenient.
- [ ] Get an early-exit clause (*turbo-clausule*): if you get a new job, you leave early and get the rest of your salary up to the end date as a lump sum.
- [ ] Ask for outplacement, courses and certifications: a budget for these is often easier to agree than money.
- [ ] Get a right of first refusal or a top-up if the role reopens within 26 weeks (Section 14).
- [ ] Ask for a short payment deadline and, if the company is moving assets out, a parent company guarantee.
- [ ] Move the payment to January if your income next year will be lower (Section 9).

### What not to do

- Don't sign while on sick leave without a lawyer: you could lose both your sick pay and your WW.
- Don't accept an end date before the notice period ends unless the gap is compensated.
- Don't sign a waiver of claims if you have a separate claim (harassment, discrimination) that the amount doesn't cover.
- Don't sign before you've put your arguments about the company's risks on the table (Section 15): after signing and the 14-day period, only narrow grounds remain.
- Don't sign an untranslated text if you don't read Dutch. Ask for an English version or a translation, and have your lawyer check that the versions match.

### Settling through mediation

If the relationship is damaged but the parties want to settle, the company may suggest mediation. The mediator runs the talks but makes no decisions and advises neither side. The company usually chooses and pays the mediator, just as it does the company doctor (*bedrijfsarts*), and the mediator may hope for its future business. Mediation usually ends with a VSO that the parties sign at the mediator's office.

- Choose a mediator from the [MfN register](https://www.mfnregister.nl): registered mediators follow professional standards and have a complaints procedure. You can turn down a mediator the company proposes and suggest another.
- Before the first meeting, agree in writing that the company pays the mediator and your lawyer.
- Before the first meeting the parties sign a mediation agreement (*mediationovereenkomst*). It usually has a confidentiality clause: what's said in the meetings can't be used in court later. The clause protects you too, but it also stops you citing in court what the company admitted in the meetings, so read it before you sign.
- The mediator isn't your lawyer and doesn't protect your interests. Bring your lawyer to the meetings or consult them between meetings, and don't sign the final text until your lawyer has checked it.
- Mediation is voluntary, and you can stop it at any time. The exception is sick leave: if the company doctor recommends mediation, refusing without a medical reason can hurt you (Section 10).
- If the company uses the meetings to pressure you or stall, tell the mediator, and if that doesn't help, stop the mediation.
- After you sign at the mediator's office, you still have 14 days to cancel the VSO.
- On a low income, you can get a state contribution to mediation (*mediationtoevoeging*): the state pays part of the costs. Check the conditions with the free legal advice service ([Het Juridisch Loket](https://www.juridischloket.nl)).

## Section 9. Money

When the employer dismisses you, the legal minimum is statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*), salary for the notice period and payment for untaken holiday. After that the state pays WW, and anything more comes out of the negotiation.

### Statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*)

[The statutory formula](https://www.recordinglaw.com/nl/netherlands/employment-law/severance-pay/) gives one third of a monthly salary per year worked, from the first day, with a partial year counted pro rata. The "monthly salary" includes the 8% holiday allowance, fixed bonuses, a 13th month and other structural payments, plus average overtime and the variable pay of recent years. Options and one-off bonuses usually aren't included.

In 2026 the cap is [EUR 102,000 gross, or one year's salary if that's higher](https://www.rendement.nl/transitievergoeding/nieuws/maximale-transitievergoeding-is-in-2026--102000.html). Most employees with a few years of service never reach it.

Take a salary of EUR 8,000 gross a month: with the 8% holiday allowance the formula uses EUR 8,640. After 3 years you get 8,640 / 3 x 3 = EUR 8,640, about one month's salary, and after 3 years and 4 months 8,640 / 3 x 3.33, about EUR 9,600. These sums are small, so treat statutory severance as the floor you negotiate up from.

You aren't owed statutory severance if you resigned or were dismissed for serious misconduct, so companies try hard to get you to leave on your own or to find you "at fault". The exception: if you resigned because of the employer's serious culpability, you're owed it.

### Notice period

The employer's statutory notice period depends on your years of service: 1 month under 5 years, 2 months for 5 to 10 years, 3 months for 10 to 15 years and 4 months from 15 years. Your contract or CAO may give more, and you're paid your salary for the whole period. By law notice runs to the end of the month unless the contract says otherwise, so the real end date is often later than it looks.

### WW benefit

WW pays 75% of your daily wage for the first 2 months and 70% after that. The daily wage it counts is capped: [from 2026-07-01 at EUR 309.91 gross a day](https://rekenmachinepro.nl/blog/ww-uitkering-hoogte-duur-berekenen/), about EUR 4,700 gross a month after the second month. Above that cap WW pays far less than 70% of your salary, so each month of full salary in a VSO is worth almost twice a month of WW.

WW lasts 3 months if you worked 26 of the last 36 weeks. If you also worked 4 of the last 5 years, WW lasts one month for each year of work history, with 3 months as the minimum: each of your first 10 years gives a month. After that, years up to 2015 give a month each and years from 2016 half a month each, up to 24 months in total. Work outside the EU doesn't count. Work in other EU and EEA countries and Switzerland can count towards both the 26 weeks and the work history for the duration, as long as your last job was in the Netherlands (article 61 of EU Regulation 883/2004). You need a [PD U1 form](https://www.uwv.nl/en/individuals/forms/pdu1-form) from that country, and check with the UWV how it will count your years. Without such periods, someone who moved to the Netherlands 3 years ago usually gets about 3 months of WW. [The cut to 12 months has been postponed to 2029](https://www.intropersoneel.nl/blog/prinsjesdag-2026-maatregelen-sociale-zaken-werkgelegenheid/).

Apply on werk.nl no later than one week after your first day without work, because the UWV may pay less if you're late. You'll need your digital government ID (DigiD), the employer's statement for WW (*werkgeversverklaring*) and the agreement or the dismissal decision.

WW is insurance you paid for with contributions from your salary, so unlike welfare it doesn't harm your residence permit. It doesn't extend the visa job-search period either. In return you must actively look for work and report to the UWV.

If you do some work while on WW, the UWV reduces the benefit but doesn't always stop it. You must report every income.

For the first six months the UWV counts work at your level of education, experience and pay, with up to 2 hours' travel a day, as suitable. After six months any job counts, including lower-level work, with up to 3 hours' travel.

### Tax, pension and the 30% ruling

- A severance payment is taxed as a special remuneration (*bijzondere beloning*). Tax is withheld at a high rate, on a high salary usually the top rate of tax on income from work (box 1), and settled in your annual return. So compare offers gross but plan your budget net.
- The payment counts as income in the year you receive it. If your income will be lower next year, for example on WW or after you leave the country, moving the payment to January may reduce the tax. Check this with a tax adviser.
- Your accrued pension rights stay yours when you're dismissed or leave the country. Partner's pension cover in case of your death usually ends with the contract or soon after, so check with your pension fund and consider private insurance for the transition period.
- The 30% ruling ends with the contract, but if your new job starts within 3 months, you can usually keep it. Check with a tax adviser: keeping the ruling can be worth more than an extra month's payout and gives you an argument for a later end date.
- The collective health insurance discount through your employer ends with the contract, but the insurance continues. Check the terms with your insurer.

## Section 10. Sick leave and layoffs

Sick leave protects you from dismissal only in some cases. The order of events decides which: whether you fell ill before or after the UWV received the dismissal application.

### How sick leave works

- You report sick to your employer without a diagnosis or details: the employer isn't entitled to know the diagnosis or ask for it. You can say when you expect to return and whether they can contact you.
- Your GP (*huisarts*) treats you and refers you to specialists but doesn't write notes for your employer, and sick leave needs no note.
- A company doctor (*bedrijfsarts*), whom the company hires through an occupational health service (*Arbodienst*), assesses your fitness for work. The company doctor tells the employer only the prognosis and your limitations, never the diagnosis, and must by law be independent. But the company pays them, and the occupational health service has a long-term contract with it, so ask for a written report after each consultation and keep your own notes of what you said.
- If you disagree with the company doctor, ask for a second opinion from another company doctor or a UWV expert opinion (*deskundigenoordeel*).
- For the first 2 years the employer pays at least 70% of your salary, and contracts often give 100% in the first year.
- Reintegration follows the Gatekeeper Improvement Act (*Wet verbetering Poortwachter*): by week 6 the company doctor writes a problem analysis, by week 8 you and your employer sign a return-to-work plan (*plan van aanpak*), and regular evaluations follow. If after a year you can't return to your own job, the second track (*tweede spoor*) begins: the search for work outside the company.

### When sick leave protects you

During the first 2 years of illness, [the employer can't dismiss you through the UWV](https://ondernemenmetpersoneel.nl/ontslaan/ontslagredenen/zieke-werknemer-ontslaan-mag-dat). The exceptions depend on when you fell ill and how the job ends:

| Situation | Protected? |
|---|---|
| You fell ill before the UWV received the application | ✅ Yes, the UWV will refuse or postpone |
| You fell ill after the UWV received the application | 🚩 [No](https://www.mkbservicedesk.nl/personeel/ontslag/het-opzegverbod-alle-situaties-en-uitzonderingen) |
| You fell ill after the application, the UWV refused, and the company goes to court | 🚩 No. [The Supreme Court ruled in 2022](https://www.hrpraktijk.nl/juridisch/ontslag/hoge-raad-ziekmelding-na-ontslagaanvraag-staat-niet-in-de-weg-aan-ontbinding-arbeidsovereenkomst/) that the exception applies in court too |
| You fell ill before the application, but the illness is expected to end within 4 weeks of the UWV decision | 🚩 No, the UWV can grant the permit, but the company can give notice only after you recover |
| The company closes its business entirely | 🚩 No |
| You sign a VSO | 🚩 No, you're agreeing yourself |

### If you're ill on the last day of your contract

If your contract ends while you're still ill, the employer stops paying your salary. You aren't entitled to WW because you can't work, so the UWV pays sickness benefit (*Ziektewet*). But if you agreed to end the contract while ill, the UWV may decide you gave up your salary voluntarily and cut the benefit. So sign a VSO on sick leave only with a lawyer, and only with a clause on what happens if you're ill on the end date.

### How sick leave affects a layoff

If you feel unwell, see a doctor and take sick leave, because that's your right. Sick leave on its own won't stop the layoff, though. The company doctor assesses your health: if they see a work conflict with no medical cause, they may declare you fit for work and suggest mediation. So describe your symptoms honestly and specifically: sleep, concentration, anxiety, workload and how they get in the way of your work.

Sick leave does strengthen your negotiating position: the employer can't dismiss a sick employee through the UWV and has to pay for up to 2 years, so the price of a VSO goes up.

Burnout during a reorganisation is a common and genuine reason for sick leave. If your work made you ill, you also have an argument that the employer is at fault, especially if the company ignored the workload (Working Conditions Act, *Arbowet*).

## Section 11. The kennismigrant visa

When you lose your job, you get a job-search period before you have to leave the country. From 2026-05-22 [its length depends on how long you've held your residence permit](https://www.everaert.nl/zoekperiode-kennismigranten-verlengd/): up to 3 months if under two years and up to 6 months if two years or more. The 6 months apply if you became unemployed on or after 2026-05-22.

### How the search period works

- The search period starts on the contract end date, so every month of notice and garden leave after your talk with HR pushes its start back.
- The search period ends no later than the expiry date on your residence card, even if that cuts it short, so check the date on your card now.
- The employer reports the end of the contract to the IND, usually within 4 weeks. Don't wait for an IND letter about the start of your search period: keep your own calendar.
- If you don't find a job during the search period, the IND may revoke your residence permit. The IND usually sends a letter of intent first, and you can respond to it.

### A new job

- Your new employer must be a recognised sponsor (*erkend referent*, listed in [the IND register](https://ind.nl/nl/openbaar-register-erkende-referenten)), and your salary must be above the highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*) threshold: in 2026 EUR 5,942 gross a month if you're 30 or older and EUR 4,357 if you're under 30.
- Your new sponsor submits a change-of-employer application to the IND. Ask them from which day you can start work.
- Check the sponsor in advance, because a company still "in the process" of getting the status is a risk for you. [Questions for the Recruiter](/articles/recruiter-guide-en) lists what to ask.

### Other ways to stay

- Permanent residence or a Dutch passport removes the search-period limit. If you're close to 5 years, the contract end date can decide a lot, so discuss it with an immigration lawyer.
- If your partner works in the Netherlands or has their own residence permit, check whether you can switch to a residence permit as their partner, which can take the time pressure off.
- If you graduated in recent years from a Dutch university or a top-ranked university abroad, you may be entitled to a separate orientation year for graduates (*zoekjaar hoogopgeleiden*). Check the conditions on the IND website.
- Since 2026-05-22 an EU Blue Card has the same search period, 3 or 6 months, so switching to one doesn't extend it.
- You can also start your own business, on a residence permit for the self-employed or, for US citizens, under the DAFT (Dutch-American Friendship Treaty). Both routes take preparation and time.

### What this means for negotiations

Do the visa maths before you agree to change the contract end date, because an offer to "leave early with a bonus" may cost you months of search time. For a highly skilled migrant the late end date is the main bargaining point: a month in employment on garden leave gives you both salary and visa time. If the case goes to court, the visa risk increases the fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*), so it raises the price of a VSO too.

## Section 12. Temporary contracts and probation

On a temporary contract your main question is whether the company will renew it. The law protects you less than on a permanent contract, but it still sets rules, and companies break them:

- Under the duty to give notice of renewal (*aanzegplicht*), the company must tell you in writing one month before a temporary contract of 6 months or more ends whether it will renew it and on what terms. If it doesn't, it owes you compensation for missing notice (*aanzegvergoeding*) of up to one month's salary, and if it tells you late, an amount in proportion to the delay. You must claim within 3 months of the breach.
- Under the chain rule (*ketenregeling*), the next contract becomes permanent after 3 temporary contracts or 3 years on temporary contracts. A gap of more than 6 months resets the chain, and some companies exploit that: they let you go and hire you again six months later. A contract through an agency for the same work at the same client often counts as a link in the chain too.
- A probation period (*proeftijd*) is valid only if it's in writing. A contract of 6 months or less can't have one, a contract longer than 6 months but shorter than 2 years can have at most one month, and a contract of 2 years or more or a permanent contract at most two months. A second probation period with the same employer is invalid unless the new job clearly needs different skills or responsibilities, and then a dismissal "during probation" is invalid too.
- During probation the company can dismiss you without a reason, but if you ask, it must give the reason in writing. The reason can't be discriminatory, such as pregnancy, illness or origin.
- A non-compete clause (*concurrentiebeding*) in a temporary contract is invalid unless the contract itself justifies it in writing with a substantial business interest (*zwaarwegend bedrijfsbelang*).
- You're owed statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) from the first day of work, also when the employer doesn't renew a temporary contract.
- If a temporary contract has no early termination clause, neither you nor the company can end it early by giving notice. What remains is an agreement, summary dismissal for an urgent cause (*dringende reden*) or dissolution by the court (*ontbinding*), so a company that wants to part ways earlier will usually have to pay.

## Section 13. How to fight your employer

In the Netherlands you win a fight with your employer through documents and procedure. The goal usually isn't to "punish the company" but to get a fair decision or good exit terms. Each tool here, from a follow-up email to a lawyer, raises what your departure costs the company.

### Document everything from day one

- After every conversation, write down the date, who was there and what was said, and send a short follow-up email: it turns a verbal conversation into evidence. For example:

  > Hello [name], I'm confirming that today, [date], we discussed [topic]. You said that [summary]. I replied that [summary] and that I'll look into the matter with a lawyer. If I've misunderstood anything, please correct me in writing.

- Keep your contract and its amendments, payslips, reviews, emails about bonuses and raises, relocation correspondence and restructuring announcements. Forward to yourself only what's yours: documents about you and addressed to you.
- Don't take company data such as code, internal documents or other people's personal data, because that gives the company grounds to dismiss you for misconduct and wrecks your position. Get what you need from internal documents through a GDPR request or through your lawyer.
- In the Netherlands you can usually record a conversation you take part in and use the recording as evidence. Sharing it is a separate legal question, so discuss it with your lawyer first.
- Don't correspond with your lawyer from a work device or work email, because under certain conditions the company can inspect them.
- Keep a timeline in one file with the date, the event and the evidence. It saves your lawyer hours and you money.

### Request your personal data under the GDPR

Under Article 15 of the GDPR (AVG in the Netherlands), you can request all the data held about you without giving a reason: your personnel file, HR's notes and correspondence that mentions you. [The company must answer within one month](https://www.rendement.nl/algemene-verordening-gegevensbescherming-avg/nieuws/organisaties-weigeren-verzoeken-om-inzage-persoonsgegevens.html), or within three months in complex cases.

Send HR and the data protection officer (DPO) a request such as this one:

> Subject: Request for access to personal data (Article 15 GDPR / AVG)
>
> I request a copy of all my personal data processed by [company], including my personnel file, assessments and reviews, notes by HR and managers, correspondence by email, Slack and Teams in which I'm mentioned, and documents related to the reorganisation and to the decision on my possible dismissal. I also request information on the purposes of the processing, the recipients of the data and the retention period. I expect your reply within one month, as Article 12 GDPR requires.

- The company redacts other people's data, and it may withhold internal legal advice: [the Supreme Court dealt with such a case in 2024](https://uitspraken.rechtspraak.nl/api/pdfdownload/ECLI_NL_HR_2024_1372).
- If the company doesn't answer or answers incompletely, you can complain to the Dutch Data Protection Authority (*Autoriteit Persoonsgegevens*) or apply to a court.
- The answer often shows how long the company has been preparing the dismissal, when and why it put you on the list and what it wrote about you, which is a strong argument in negotiations.
- Send the request early, because the month the company has to reply can eat up the whole negotiation period.

### Internal channels

- A confidential adviser (*vertrouwenspersoon*) on harassment and inappropriate behaviour helps with a complaint and passes nothing on without your consent. The law doesn't yet require a company to appoint one. But the Labour Authority (*Nederlandse Arbeidsinspectie*) treats one as part of a policy against psychosocial workload, so a missing adviser supports an argument that the policy is weak.
- File a formal complaint under the internal procedure and stick to facts: the date, what happened and the witnesses, with no judgements or emotions. The complaint sets a date from which any retaliation is later measured.
- If many people are affected, the works council (OR) can demand information and influence the process.

### External channels

- Under the Working Conditions Act (*Arbowet*), the employer must [have a policy against psychosocial workload](https://www.awvn.nl/ongewenst-gedrag/), covering harassment, intimidation, discrimination and excessive workload. The Labour Authority can investigate the company, demand corrections or fine it. The inspectorate won't resolve your individual case, but it puts pressure on the company, and you can report to it anonymously.
- The Whistleblowers Authority (*Huis voor Klokkenluiders*) takes reports of serious wrongdoing in the public interest, and before you report you can get confidential advice there for free. The Whistleblower Protection Act (*Wet bescherming klokkenluiders*) prohibits retaliation for a report made in good faith.
- If the selection for the layoff shows signs of discrimination, turn to the Netherlands Institute for Human Rights (*College voor de Rechten van de Mens*). It's free, and you don't need a lawyer.
- Union membership (FNV, CNV, De Unie) includes legal help, but not for conflicts that started before you joined. CNV also has a 3-month waiting period; under its terms, FNV has none. So join before a conflict starts. Unions also negotiate the social plan (*sociaal plan*).
- If many people are affected, act together: one lawyer, one position on the social plan and a joint claim. A company finds it harder to pressure a group than individuals, and you share the lawyer's costs.

### How to find a lawyer

- Look for an employment lawyer (*arbeidsrechtadvocaat*), preferably a member of VAAN (the association of employment lawyers). For your visa you need a separate immigration lawyer.
- The first consultation is often free or inexpensive, and lawyers often handle a VSO for a fixed fee, which the company pays under the agreement.
- Many lawyers in the larger cities work in English, so ask about the language straight away.
- If you have legal expenses insurance (*rechtsbijstandsverzekering*), call your insurer first: it may assign its own lawyer or pay for yours. Policies usually have a waiting period of 3 to 6 months and don't cover a conflict that has already started.

### What not to do

- Don't write emotional emails or make threats: anything in writing can end up in the case file.
- Don't post accusations with names on social media or in chats: you risk a defamation claim and give the company a reason to build a "broken working relationship" ground (Section 6).
- Don't discuss your strategy with anyone who might pass it on to the company.
- Don't stop working without sick leave or garden leave: that's unauthorised absence and grounds for dismissal without payouts.

## Section 14. The company's legal risks

A layoff in the Netherlands goes through a chain of mandatory steps: the OR's advice, the WMCO notification, the waiting month, a UWV permit with the right selection order (*afspiegeling*) and a real redeployment effort (*herplaatsing*), and the ban on rehiring for the same job. Every skipped or badly done step gives the employee the right to go to court.

Few cases reach court, because the company and its lawyers know a court case may cost more than a generous settlement now. Your move builds on that calculation:

1. Find specific mistakes and weak points in the procedure.
2. Assess each one: what a court could do, and how likely that is.
3. Through your lawyer, show the company you see the mistakes and will go further if the terms don't change.

### Risk map

WMCO breaches and rehiring for the same job cost the company most, because they can undo the dismissal entirely. The other mistakes usually turn into money.

| Company mistake | What a court can do | What you need to show | Your deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| No WMCO notification, no union consultation, waiting month not observed | [Annul both the dismissal and a signed VSO](https://www.awvn.nl/reorganiseren/hr-van-a-tot-z/wet-melding-collectief-ontslag/). You're employed again and the company owes salary for the whole period | 20+ dismissals within 3 months in one UWV region, VSOs included, and the breach of procedure | 6 months after the dismissal or the VSO signing |
| A VSO signed during the waiting month | The same: [a VSO can't be concluded during that month either](https://www.uwv.nl/werkgevers/werkgever-en-ontslag/ik-wil-ontslag-aanvragen/detail/collectief-ontslag) | The date the UWV notification was complete and the date of signing | 6 months after signing |
| Someone else hired for the same work within 26 weeks of the dismissal, without offering it to you | [Reinstate you or award fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*)](https://www.uwv.nl/nl/ontslag/opnieuw-in-dienst) | The vacancy or the new hire with the same tasks | 2 months after the 26 weeks end |
| The work moved to another group company and the people were laid off | Find a transfer of undertaking (*overgang van onderneming*): your contract moves to the new employer, and dismissal because of the transfer is prohibited | The same work, clients, contracts or revenue at another group company | Talk to your lawyer immediately |
| Wrong selection order: the wrong pool of interchangeable positions (*uitwisselbare functies*), a "unique" role that isn't unique | At the UWV: refusal of the permit. After a permit, the court (subdistrict court, *kantonrechter*) can reinstate the employee or award fair compensation | Who else does the same work, their age and service | UWV objection about 2 weeks; court 2 months after the contract ends |
| No real redeployment effort, including across the group | The same as for a wrong selection order | Open group vacancies you qualified for | The same |
| A weak or artificial economic case | UWV refusal; after a permit, reinstatement or fair compensation. The court treats false information in the application as serious culpability (*ernstig verwijtbaar*) | That the work continues under another name or moves to contractors, or that transfer prices created the loss | The same |
| Selecting people who complained or criticised | Annul the dismissal or award fair compensation for prohibited retaliation | The timeline: the complaint, then your name on the list | 2 months after the contract ends |
| Dismissing an OR member or candidate without the court's consent | Annul the dismissal | Your OR status | 2 months after the contract ends |
| Notice period not observed | Salary for the missing period | The dates | 2 months after the contract ends |
| Reorganisation without the OR's advice or against it | The OR can appeal the decision to the Enterprise Chamber ([*Ondernemingskamer*](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/roadmap-reorganisatie-3-het-beroepsrecht-van-de-ondernemingsraad)), which can order the consequences reversed. The UWV won't process applications without completed advice | How the advice procedure went | For the OR, 1 month after being told of the decision |

The deadline for a court claim is [a strict limitation period (*vervaltermijn*)](https://www.hrpraktijk.nl/juridisch/wet-en-regelgeving/checklist-wwz-procestermijnen/). A letter doesn't interrupt it and it can't be extended, so missing it by one day loses you the right.

### What a court can do

When a layoff is unlawful, the court can annul the dismissal, order the contract restored or award fair compensation. Retroactive annulment costs the company most, because the debt grows every month the case runs.

#### Annulment (*vernietiging*)

The court annuls the dismissal if the company broke a mandatory rule: the WMCO, a dismissal ban (*opzegverbod*) or the permit requirement. The contract then counts as never having ended, and the company owes salary for the whole period. For late payment the law allows an increase of up to 50% plus interest.

#### Reinstatement (*herstel*)

Sometimes the UWV wrongly grants a permit, because of a wrong selection order, no redeployment effort or a weak business case. The court can then order the company to restore the contract, including from a past date with back pay for the gap.

#### Fair compensation

The court awards fair compensation as money in place of reinstatement, or as compensation for the employer's serious culpability. There's no formula, and the court weighs the circumstances, [including the New Hairstyle criteria](https://www.dirkzwager.nl/kennis/artikelen/billijke-vergoeding/). It looks at how much longer you'd probably have worked there, how much income you'll lose while job-hunting, how serious the breach was and what you've already received, including the statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*). For a highly skilled migrant (*kennismigrant*), courts also weigh the risk of losing the residence permit, and that risk increases the damage.

### Deadlines and the course of a case

| Step | Deadline |
|---|---|
| Objection to the UWV | About 2 weeks after the UWV letter |
| Creditor objection in a legal demerger (*splitsing*) | 1 month after the announcement |
| OR appeal to the Enterprise Chamber | 1 month after being told of the decision |
| Claim for a WMCO breach | 6 months after the dismissal or the VSO signing |
| Court claim after a UWV-permit dismissal | 2 months after the contract ends |
| Claim for statutory severance | 3 months after the contract ends |
| Claim over rehiring for your job | 2 months after the 26 weeks end |
| Hearing in court | Usually several months |
| Appeal | More months; the first-instance ruling may change |

Court costs in employment cases are usually small next to the amounts at stake, but each side generally pays its own lawyer. A union or legal expenses insurance (*rechtsbijstandsverzekering*) bought in advance often covers the lawyer's bill.

## Section 15. Turning risk into money

A good settlement costs the company slightly less than its best alternative plus the price of its risk. So count what it costs the company not to settle, not what you want: that cost sets what the company is ready to pay.

### What goes into the price

| What to count | Why the company pays for it |
|---|---|
| Statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*) | The legal minimum on every route except your own resignation |
| Salary during the UWV or court procedure | While the procedure runs, the contract continues and salary is paid |
| Notice period | Paid in any case |
| The company's lawyers | A UWV or court procedure costs the company dozens of lawyer hours |
| Risk of a UWV or court refusal | After a refusal the company starts over and pays for longer |
| Risk of fair compensation (*billijke vergoeding*) | For serious culpability, the court awards a payment above the minimum |
| WMCO risk | A breach of the collective redundancy procedure gives grounds to annul the dismissal and the VSO |
| Sick leave before the UWV application | Up to 2 years of pay with no way to dismiss through the UWV |
| Reputation and time | A public court case and a dragged-out reorganisation cost money too |

### The formula

The expected cost to the company of not settling = the unavoidable costs of the procedure + the sum of each risk x its probability. A reasonable settlement lies between your minimum and that sum.

### A worked example

All figures are illustrative. Your lawyer estimates the probabilities from the facts of your case.

The inputs: a salary of EUR 8,000 gross a month, EUR 8,640 with holiday allowance, 3 years of service. The company needs 20+ redundancies and is rushing the procedure.

| Item | Calculation | Cost to the company, EUR |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory severance | 8,640 / 3 x 3 years | 8,640 |
| Extra salary: the WMCO waiting month and a UWV procedure in place of a quick VSO | about 2 months x 8,640 | 17,280 |
| The company's lawyers for the UWV and court | Assumption | 10,000 |
| Risk of fair compensation over a disputed selection order (*afspiegeling*) | 20% x 6 months (51,840) | 10,368 |
| Risk of WMCO annulment with a year's pay and the increase | 10% x 12 months x 1.5 (155,520) | 15,552 |
| **Expected cost to the company** | | **about 61,800** |

The company's first offer often equals the statutory severance (EUR 8,640). EUR 61,800 is the optimistic case, for a company that rushes and makes mistakes, and it comes with two caveats. The 1.5 multiplier in the last row is the maximum statutory increase for late salary (7:625 BW), and courts often reduce it. While the UWV procedure runs, you keep working, so the salary for that time isn't a pure loss to the company unless it has released you from work.

### A conservative case

A careful company notified the UWV under the WMCO in advance, consulted the OR and got the selection order right. Then the risks are gone, and only the unavoidable costs remain:

| Item | Calculation | Cost to the company, EUR |
|---|---|---|
| Statutory severance | 8,640 / 3 x 3 years | 8,640 |
| The company's lawyers for the UWV | Assumption | 10,000 |
| Salary during the procedure, if you're released from work | 0 to 2 months x 8,640 | 0 to 17,280 |
| **Expected cost to the company** | | **about 18,600 to 35,900** |

In this case a reasonable target is 2 to 4 times the statutory severance, not seven. An amount closer to EUR 61,800 is realistic only if you and your lawyer have found specific mistakes in the procedure.

### How to read the calculation

The company pays the unavoidable costs from the conservative case every time, even after a flawless procedure, so any employee can rely on them. The risks work only with facts: 20% with no specific mistake in the selection order is a bluff, and the company's lawyer will see it. The risks that undo the dismissal cost most: one real WMCO argument can outweigh five weak ones.

You pay too, in lawyer's fees, time, stress and the risk of losing, so the final amount is usually below the full cost to the company. Aim for the middle of the zone between the first offer and the cost to the company as the facts of your case put it.

### Market benchmarks

The legal minimum is statutory severance: one third of a monthly salary per year worked. One legal website [cites an average](https://webwoordenboek.nl/kenniscentrum/heb-ik-recht-op-transitievergoeding-bij-vaststellingsovereenkomst) of about 0.57 of a monthly salary per year worked in a VSO, but gives no data or date, so treat it as a rough and possibly dated benchmark.

In my experience and that of people I know, with one to five years of service companies in large layoffs often offer about 6 months' salary in total. I have no data behind this number, and it depends on length of service. After one year, 3 months can be a fair offer if the procedure has no weak spots. With a few years of service and a rushed procedure, 3 months is a lowball first offer, made in the hope that you won't negotiate. With long service the benchmark changes: after 18 years the statutory severance alone equals 6 months' salary, so compare the offer with the statutory severance and count how many months the company adds on top.

The old subdistrict court formula A x B x C (*kantonrechtersformule*: weighted years of service x salary x correction factor) has been formally abolished but [is still used as a benchmark](https://www.rocketlawyer.com/nl/nl/personeel/ontslag-geven/rechtwijzer/verschillende-berekenmethodes-ontslagvergoeding) in negotiations. C = 1 means a neutral case, and C goes above 1 when the employer is at fault.

With short service, count in months of salary: with 3 years of service, statutory severance is one month's pay, and a reasonable negotiation covers several months on top for the procedure time, the risks and the visa.

### How to use the risks in negotiations

A risk puts pressure on the company only once it knows you see it. So gather the facts first, then send a lawyer's letter with the calculation, and only then name a number.

#### 1. Ask questions in writing

Send HR a short, neutral email. Each answer removes a risk or records it, and a refusal to answer stays in the correspondence, where your lawyer can use it too.

- Has a collective redundancy notification been filed with the UWV and the unions? When did the UWV confirm it was complete?
- Was the OR asked for advice on the reorganisation? Was the advice received, and does the decision follow it?
- Which pool of interchangeable positions was used for the selection order? Which age group am I in, and where do I rank?
- Which group vacancies were considered for my redeployment?
- Who will do my work after the layoff?

At the same time, send a GDPR data request: the answer often shows when and why you were put on the list.

#### 2. Bring in your lawyer

Your lawyer, in a businesslike tone and without threats, lists the specific weak points, cites the rules, names the consequences and proposes settlement terms. The letter shows the company that a formal procedure comes next.

#### 3. Use collective leverage

WMCO, OR and selection-order risks affect everyone being laid off at once. If ten people with one lawyer raise the same question, the company's risk multiplies by ten. The shared risk also gives you grounds to discuss a social plan (*sociaal plan*) through the union.

#### 4. Protect your leverage in the agreement

A signed VSO with a full waiver of claims usually closes most of the risks, so agree on protections before you sign. The 26-week rehiring rule (Section 14) applies only to dismissal with a UWV permit. A VSO can recreate it by contract: a top-up or a right of first refusal if the role reopens within 26 weeks.

WMCO annulment also covers a VSO signed in breach, so if in doubt, check the dates with your lawyer. Put your arguments on the table before you sign: after signing and the 14-day period (Section 8) only narrow grounds remain.

#### 5. Watch the vacancies after the dismissal

If you were dismissed through the UWV, watch the company's and the group's vacancies for 26 weeks and screenshot ads with the same tasks. Rehiring for your job is a separate ground for a claim (Section 14), even if everything else went cleanly.

### The negotiation, step by step

1. At the HR meeting, listen, take the written offer, and sign and promise nothing. Say: "Thank you, I'll review this with my lawyer."
2. Build your position: send the GDPR request and written questions to HR, calculate the money and deadlines, and draw up the company's risk map.
3. Have your lawyer send the counteroffer, in writing and with arguments: from a lawyer it carries more weight.
4. Negotiations usually take 2 to 4 rounds. The company almost always improves its first offer, and the final amount is often well above the starting one.
5. If the money hits a ceiling, negotiate the whole package: the end date, garden leave (*vrijstelling van werk*), the bonus, the lawyer's fee and the reference. [Practitioners note](https://www.bruto-netto.nl/vaststellingsovereenkomst-onderhandelen-ontslagvergoeding/) that negotiating several items often yields more than one amount.
6. Check the final text: WW-proof, the end date, the payment deadline, the waiver of claims, confidentiality and a mutual non-disparagement clause.
7. Sign the agreement, and remember the 14 days to cancel (Section 8).

### Tactics

- While you're employed, time works for you: your salary keeps coming and the company needs to finish the reorganisation, so HR's deadlines press on the company harder than on you.
- Anchor high and tie the number to the cost of the procedure and the risks: the company easily dismisses a number with no arguments.
- Settlement negotiations between lawyers are confidential: a lawyer can't show them to the court without the other side's lawyer's consent ([rule 27 of the Gedragsregels advocatuur](https://www.advocatenorde.nl/voor-advocaten/wet-en-regelgeving/gedragsregels/gedragsregels-advocatuur/de-advocaat-de-1)). Your own emails to HR fall outside this rule and are ordinary evidence, so negotiate through your lawyer and don't write anything yourself that you're not prepared to see in the case file.
- Compare offers with colleagues, if they're willing to share: companies often make everyone the same first offer, and the full picture shows how much room the company has.
- If the relationship is damaged but both sides want to settle, try mediation (Section 8). The mediator is neutral, and the company usually pays.
- You can settle after the dismissal too. If you have claims, you negotiate under the threat of a lawsuit, so watch the two-month deadline (Section 14).
- Put the payment deadline in the agreement, for example 30 days after the contract ends, or the money may be delayed.

### The limits of the move

The move works against a company that's in a hurry and skimping on lawyers. Against a careful reorganisation it gets you only the unavoidable costs: build that outcome into your calculation from the start.

- Good company lawyers close the risks in advance: a large company will probably file the WMCO notification, run the OR advice procedure and wait the month. That leaves the selection order, redeployment and the business case, which are weaker arguments and need facts.
- Courts respect the decision to reorganise: they check whether the procedure was lawful, not whether closing a business line is sensible. In August 2026 the Enterprise Chamber ([*Ondernemingskamer*](https://www.dutchitchannel.nl/news/762291/ondernemingskamer-keurt-reorganisatie-amazon-goed)) rejected an OR's appeal against Amazon's reorganisation, despite disputed economics.
- In practice courts rarely reinstate and more often award money, and fair compensation is unpredictable and can be modest.
- If your role truly has no equivalent, the selection order doesn't apply to it and the selection-order argument won't work.
- A threat without facts, or a missed deadline, shows you have no leverage, and later offers get worse.
- You pay too: your lawyer's fee if no union or insurance covers it, months of uncertainty and, if you refuse the VSO, the risk of ending up with only the legal minimum.
- The deadlines are strict limitation periods: 2 months for most claims and 6 months under the WMCO (Section 14). Once you miss one, the threat of a claim is worth nothing.

The company pays for the risk that you and your lawyer have calculated and shown: the more precisely you name the procedure's weak points and what each costs, the more it costs the company not to settle.

## Section 16. What to do right now

Take these steps before your first conversation with HR: your access may be cut off on the day.

### While nothing has been announced

- [ ] Save your contract, appendices, the last year's payslips, reviews and emails about bonuses, shares and relocation
- [ ] Save personal contact details of colleagues who can vouch for your work
- [ ] Calculate your statutory severance (*transitievergoeding*), notice period and approximate WW
- [ ] Check your residence permit's expiry date and how long you've held it: it decides whether you get 3 or 6 months to search
- [ ] Find an employment lawyer (*arbeidsrechtadvocaat*) or join a union
- [ ] Check your legal expenses insurance (*rechtsbijstandsverzekering*): whether you have it, its terms and waiting period
- [ ] Update your CV and start looking, even if you end up staying
- [ ] If you feel unwell, see your GP (*huisarts*) now, without waiting for news
- [ ] If the company is restructuring the group, check its KvK filings (Section 7)

### When you're called to a meeting

- [ ] Listen, ask questions, sign nothing
- [ ] Ask for the offer in writing
- [ ] Right after the meeting, write down what was said and send a confirmation email
- [ ] Send a GDPR data request
- [ ] Ask HR in writing about the WMCO, the OR's advice, the selection pool, redeployment vacancies and who will do your work

### When you have a written offer

- [ ] Give it to your lawyer before you reply to the company
- [ ] Build the company's risk map and the calculation with your lawyer (Sections 14 and 15)
- [ ] Check WW-proof, the end date and notice period, your visa, the waiver of claims and the payment deadline
- [ ] Send a counteroffer through your lawyer
- [ ] After signing, remember the 14 days to cancel

### If the company goes to the UWV

- [ ] Note the date of the UWV letter
- [ ] Prepare your objection: selection order, interchangeable positions, group vacancies, the group's accounts and transfer prices
- [ ] After the decision, keep track of the two-month deadline for a claim

### After you leave

- [ ] Apply for WW on werk.nl within a week
- [ ] Check the final settlement: holiday allowance, untaken days, bonus
- [ ] Watch your former employer's and the group's vacancies for your role for 26 weeks
- [ ] Note the end date of your visa job-search period
- [ ] Put every deadline in your calendar: 2 months, 3 months, 6 months under the WMCO

| Article | What it says |
|---|---|

| 7:611a BW | The company can't claw back the cost of training that the law or a CAO makes mandatory. |
| 7:613 BW | The employer can change your working conditions unilaterally only if the contract provides for it and the employer's interest outweighs yours. |
| 7:625 BW | For late salary, the employer pays a statutory increase of up to 50%. |
| 7:641 BW | Untaken holiday is paid out when you leave. |
| 7:656 BW | The employer must give you a certificate of employment (*getuigschrift*) on request. |
| 7:662 BW onwards | In a transfer of undertaking (*overgang van onderneming*), employees move to the new employer automatically; dismissal because of the transfer itself is prohibited. |
| 7:668 BW | *Aanzegplicht*: for a temporary contract of 6 months or more, the company must say in writing a month before the end whether it'll renew. |
| 7:669 BW | Dismissal at the employer's initiative needs a valid reason (*redelijke grond*) from a closed list and proof that redeployment isn't possible. |
| 7:670 BW | Dismissal bans (*opzegverbod*): during illness, pregnancy, OR membership and in other cases. |
| 7:670b BW | 14 days to cancel a termination agreement. |
| 7:672 BW | Notice periods. |
| 7:673 BW | *Transitievergoeding*: the minimum severance payment for dismissal at the employer's initiative. |
| 7:682 BW | If a dismissal permit was wrongly granted, the court reinstates the contract or awards a *billijke vergoeding*. The article also sets the rule on rehiring within 26 weeks. |
| 7:686a BW | Claim deadline: 2 months for most claims, 3 months for the transitievergoeding. These are strict limitation periods (*vervaltermijn*). |

### Free speech and protection against retaliation

| Article or law | What it says |
|---|---|
| Article 7 of the Grondwet (the Constitution) and Article 10 of the ECHR (European Convention on Human Rights) | Protect freedom of expression, including about your employer, within the duty to be a good employee. |
| Wet bescherming klokkenluiders | Prohibits disadvantaging a whistleblower; the burden of proof is on the company. |
| AWGB, WGBH/CZ | Prohibit disadvantaging anyone who complained about discrimination. |

### Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 2 (Book 2: legal entities)

| Article | What it says |
|---|---|
| 2:216 BW | Before a B.V. pays out to shareholders, the board checks that the company can still pay its debts after the distribution. Otherwise the directors are personally liable. |
| 2:334a BW onwards | In a legal demerger (*splitsing*, *afsplitsing*), creditors can object, and after the demerger both companies are jointly liable for the debts. |
| 2:403 BW | Under a 403 declaration, the parent company is liable for a subsidiary's debts, and the subsidiary may skip publishing full accounts. |

### Burgerlijk Wetboek Boek 3

Article 3:45 BW (*actio pauliana*) lets you challenge transactions that harm creditors, especially in insolvency.

### Wet melding collectief ontslag (WMCO)

If a company dismisses 20 or more people within 3 months in one UWV region, it must notify the UWV and the unions and wait a month. If it breaches this rule, you have 6 months to annul the dismissal and the VSO.

### Wet op de ondernemingsraden (WOR)

A company with 50 or more people must have an OR (works council).

| Article | What it says |
|---|---|
| Article 25 | The OR has a right of advice (*adviesrecht*) on reorganisations, changes of control and major changes to the organisation. The company must ask while the advice can still affect the decision. |
| Article 26 | The OR can appeal a decision to the Ondernemingskamer within one month. |

### Werkloosheidswet (WW)

The Unemployment Insurance Act (*Werkloosheidswet*) sets the amount and duration of unemployment benefit and the duty to look for work.

### Algemene verordening gegevensbescherming (AVG / GDPR)

Article 15 of the AVG gives the right to request all your personal data, including your personnel file and HR's notes.
